# ATHENA Wave 09 — Integrated Project System
Version 1.0 · 2026-11-19 · Working draft

## Purpose

Wave 09 turns ATHENA's existing integrated-project material into a coherent project system. It preserves legacy projects and their IDs, while establishing exactly three distinct classes for new projects.

**The central rule:** classify a project by why it exists and what success means—not by its subject, tool, discipline or output format.

## The three project classes

### Personal-use projects

**Purpose:** Solve an actual problem, need or friction in the learner's own life, study, sport, creative workflow, household role or personal system.

**Success test:** The learner's original problem is measurably or demonstrably better addressed; the learner can explain the change and its limits.

**Must have**
- A real learner-owned problem observed or stated before solution design
- A defined user (normally the learner) and context
- A baseline or current-state description
- A practical artefact, process or demonstrated change
- A review of whether the solution actually helps

**Must not require**
- Sale or monetisation
- Public release or audience approval
- A client or external customer
- A polished product or commercial viability

**Valid stop conditions**
- Problem turns out to be unimportant
- Existing solution is already adequate
- Trial causes extra burden or risk
- A smaller non-digital change solves it better

### Product/business-foundation projects

**Purpose:** Build something useful with a credible path to later improvement, packaging, distribution and potential sale, while testing assumptions before committing resources.

**Success test:** A bounded product/service prototype or operational foundation is tested against a real task, with evidence about problem fit, quality, delivery, cost, capacity, risk and next decision.

**Must have**
- A defined user/problem hypothesis
- A useful artefact or service prototype
- Testable acceptance criteria and failure cases
- Basic operational and unit-economics reasoning appropriate to scope
- Privacy, safety, legal/platform and ethical boundaries
- An explicit continue/change/stop decision

**Must not require**
- A sale
- Revenue or profit
- Public launch
- Paid tools or hosting
- Premature company/legal structure
- Claims of product-market fit from small samples

**Valid stop conditions**
- Insufficient problem evidence
- Delivery cost/capacity is not viable
- Risk or legal/platform conditions are unsuitable
- Prototype fails the core user task
- A different approach is more proportionate

### Intrinsic/for-its-own-sake projects

**Purpose:** Explore, investigate, make, play, compose, collect, experiment or create because the subject itself is interesting or meaningful to the learner.

**Success test:** The learner has genuinely explored a chosen question or creative direction and can describe observations, choices, discoveries, uncertainty or new curiosity.

**Must have**
- A learner-chosen curiosity, fascination, expressive intention or puzzle
- Freedom to follow unexpected directions
- Some trace of exploration, iteration or observation
- A reflection on what was discovered or what remains open

**Must not require**
- Practical utility
- Commercial potential
- Customer demand
- A public audience or publication
- A finished/polished artefact
- Transfer to another discipline

**Valid stop conditions**
- Curiosity is satisfied
- The direction becomes uninteresting
- The exploration reveals a better question
- The learner chooses to pause or abandon it

## Classification rules

- Do not classify by subject matter alone; classify by the project's primary reason for existing and its success condition.
- A digital app can be personal-use if it solves the learner's own problem; product/business-foundation only if it is designed and tested as a useful offer for a defined user beyond the learner.
- A research project can be intrinsic exploration if driven by curiosity; personal-use if it informs an actual personal decision; product/business-foundation if it tests a product/service opportunity.
- An art project remains intrinsic when no client or utility is required, even if the finished work could later be sold.
- Projects may produce transferable skills, but transfer is evidence to capture, not a requirement imposed on intrinsic projects.
- Teamwork, research, design, software, writing and public presentation are methods or formats, not additional project classes.
- Never merge the three classes into one generic project template. Shared metadata may exist, but purpose, success criteria, risk model and assessment emphasis remain class-specific.

## Project selection decision procedure

1. Ask why the learner wants to do this project.
2. Identify the primary beneficiary and success condition.
3. If the primary beneficiary is the learner and success means improving their own real context, choose personal_use.
4. If the purpose is to build a useful offer/system for a defined user and test its future delivery/commercial path, choose product_business_foundation.
5. If the purpose is curiosity, expression, experimentation or creation without a utility requirement, choose intrinsic_exploration.
6. If more than one purpose is genuinely present, split into separate linked projects or choose one primary class and record secondary motivations; do not blend class criteria.

## Shared lifecycle, different success criteria

All projects can use a lightweight lifecycle: intent → scope → objectives/evidence → resources → milestones → execution → check → revise/stop → reflection. The lifecycle is shared; the class-specific design and evaluation are not.

| Dimension | Personal-use | Product/business-foundation | Intrinsic exploration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Real personal friction/need | User/problem hypothesis | Curiosity/creative intention |
| Primary beneficiary | Learner / personal context | Defined user and possible future operator | Learner's curiosity and expression |
| Success | Actual improvement | Useful tested foundation and justified next decision | Meaningful exploration/discovery |
| Commercial path | Not required | Possible future path, not assumed | Not required and not a success criterion |
| Public audience | Optional | Optional; test users may be appropriate | Optional |
| Failure/stop | Solution does not help | Evidence says change/stop | Curiosity changes or is satisfied |
| Transfer | Useful but separately evidenced | Useful but separately evidenced | Optional, never required |

## Resource discovery and study

1. Begin with the project's selected module objectives and the learner's actual gap.
2. Search for resources that directly teach or enable those objectives.
3. Compare relevance, authority, level, currency, safety, accessibility and licensing.
4. Use only the relevant material; explain it in ATHENA's own teaching structure and add aligned practice.
5. Keep source provenance. A link alone is not a lesson, and a resource register is not a whitelist.
6. Paid material may be optional enrichment; do not make it a mandatory dependency if an accessible substitute teaches the same outcome.
7. Do not copy restricted course content or assets.

## Evidence and assessment

Keep four things separate:
- **Project quality:** whether the project was appropriately scoped, executed and evaluated.
- **Module competence:** whether the learner independently meets a selected module's own assessment construct.
- **Transfer:** whether the learner applies a capability in a distinct context and explains what changed.
- **Retention:** whether capability remains available after a meaningful delay.

A polished artefact, project completion, time spent, public praise or AI confidence does not automatically establish competence or mastery. Use the Wave 08 instrument only when the project activity genuinely elicits its construct.

## Scope and workload

- Start with the smallest complete version that answers the driving question.
- Use flexible milestones, not fixed time quotas or streaks.
- A phone-only, no-cost or paper prototype is a valid starting point.
- Expand only when evidence or intrinsic curiosity justifies it.
- Projects do not create automatic backlog; pausing or stopping is a valid decision.
- Project work must not displace school, sleep, recovery or safety.

## Safety, privacy and age-aware boundaries

- Public launch, spending, contracts, paid services, payment handling and real customer data require guardian involvement and applicable legal/platform checks.
- Use synthetic or anonymised data by default.
- No unauthorised system testing, scraping, spam, deceptive marketing or unsupported guarantees.
- Electronics work stays within verified safe low-voltage educational setups; no mains electricity, improvised high-energy sources or unsupervised soldering.
- Physical training projects must be technique-led, recovery-aware and stopped for pain or concerning symptoms.
- Household projects exclude hazardous chemicals, electrical/gas/plumbing repairs and changes to shared property without permission.
- Creative work must respect copyright, privacy and consent.

## Legacy portfolio treatment

All nine legacy integrated portfolio IDs and all 24 entries in the two earlier project-bank batches are preserved and receive a provisional class mapping in the migration crosswalk. Some mappings are conditional because a project title alone cannot establish the learner's primary intent.

- The nine `ATH-IP-M14-*` portfolio records retain their IDs. `ATH-IP-M14-009` remains a collaboration format, not a fourth class.
- The earlier `P-01`–`P-12` and `IP-013`–`IP-024` records retain their original content and IDs.
- Conditional records must be classified by actual purpose at the point of use. For example, a research project can be intrinsic when curiosity-led or personal-use when it informs a real personal decision.
- If a legacy project's primary purpose does not fit any of the three definitions, do not force it into a category; redesign its purpose or keep it outside this three-class project bank.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-001 — Personal Knowledge and Study Retrieval System

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Solve the learner's own problem of scattered notes and unreliable retrieval by building a small personal system that makes useful knowledge findable and testable.

**Driving question:** Can I reliably find, explain and retrieve the knowledge I actually need without creating a complicated system?

**Disciplines:** WORK, RES, ENG, PSY, LANG, AEST  
**Target modules:** BASE-WORK-002, BASE-WORK-001, BASE-RES-005, BASE-RES-011, BASE-PSY-003, BASE-ENG-001, BASE-AEST-003

**Milestones**
1. **Observe the real problem:** Record 5–10 genuine moments when a note or fact was hard to find; identify the most costly failure, not an imagined feature list.
2. **Define the minimum model:** Choose a small number of note types and fields; define naming, stable identity, source provenance and one relationship type.
3. **Build a tiny working collection:** Enter 10–20 useful notes from current study or projects; keep original source details and distinguish quotation from paraphrase.
4. **Test retrieval and navigation:** Ask 5 real questions; measure whether the answer can be found and explain any failed search.
5. **Revise and test after delay:** Change one design weakness; after a delay, retrieve and explain selected ideas without reading the notes first.

**Deliverables**
- A personal use-case and friction log
- A small structured knowledge collection with stable IDs and source provenance
- A searchable or navigable index (paper, spreadsheet or local digital format)
- Retrieval prompts and a delayed-recall record
- A short change log and reflection

**Acceptance criteria**
- Solves a documented personal retrieval problem rather than merely looking organised.
- A note can be found from a realistic query and traced to its source where applicable.
- Retrieval prompts test understanding rather than recognition alone.
- The system remains small enough to maintain; unused complexity is removed.
- Delayed retrieval is reported honestly and not confused with app usage.

**Evidence**
- Initial friction observations
- Data model and sample records
- Search/retrieval test log
- Before/after revision diff
- Delayed recall attempt and explanation

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Use plain text/Markdown, a notes app or a spreadsheet; no custom app required.
- low_resource: Paper index cards with stable IDs and a source ledger.
- expanded: Build a local-first searchable interface only after the manual model proves useful.

**Safety and ethics**
- Do not store passwords, recovery codes, private messages or sensitive personal data.
- Use local/offline storage; do not upload private notes to public AI services.
- No need to collect data about other people.

**Project quality rubric**
- problem_fit: Problem is observed, bounded and personally meaningful.
- information_integrity: Identity, provenance and distinction between fact/inference/opinion are maintained.
- retrieval_performance: A changed query can retrieve relevant information and learner can explain it.
- maintenance: The system is sustainable, portable and not over-engineered.

**Completion rule:** The learner can solve the original retrieval problem on multiple real examples, explain the design trade-offs and reproduce the system from its documented structure.

**Transfer:** Apply the same capture-and-retrieval method to a different knowledge domain and identify what should change.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-002 — Personal Football Performance Feedback Loop

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Improve one chosen football performance weakness using observable evidence, safe practice design and recovery-aware iteration.

**Driving question:** Which controllable football action should I practise next, and what evidence would show useful improvement?

**Disciplines:** ATH, BODY, MATH, PSY, RES, WORK  
**Target modules:** BASE-ATH-009, BASE-ATH-012, BASE-ATH-010, BASE-ATH-011, BASE-MATH-005, BASE-RES-008, BASE-PSY-008

**Milestones**
1. **Select one observable behaviour:** Choose one skill or decision (e.g. first touch direction, scanning before receiving, pass choice); avoid vague goals such as 'get better'.
2. **Capture a baseline:** Use a small number of comparable attempts; record context, not just success/failure; avoid filming others without permission.
3. **Design a safe practice constraint:** Choose low-risk drills matched to current ability; define effort, rest and stop signals; do not add volume to chase a result.
4. **Practise and record:** Use brief sessions; record process cues and quality; stop for pain, dizziness, unusual breathlessness or injury concerns.
5. **Compare and adapt:** Compare like with like, identify uncertainty and choose one next adjustment; retain recovery as part of the result.

**Deliverables**
- A narrow performance question and baseline observation
- A simple action/cue observation rubric
- A short practice plan with warm-up, workload and recovery boundaries
- A practice log with contextual notes
- A post-practice comparison and next-step decision

**Acceptance criteria**
- The target is a controllable behaviour with an observable definition.
- Baseline and follow-up use sufficiently similar conditions and note confounders.
- Practice is age-appropriate, technique-led and includes rest.
- The conclusion does not claim causation from a tiny sample.
- The next action follows evidence and personal readiness, not pressure.

**Evidence**
- Target definition
- Observation rubric
- Practice plan and safety boundary
- Dated log with context
- Comparison and reflection

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Use a paper/phone log; a friend may observe only with consent.
- low_resource: No equipment beyond a ball and safe open space; adapt to available ground.
- expanded: Use short clips of the learner only, if guardian permission and privacy are appropriate.

**Safety and ethics**
- No maximal testing, punishment conditioning or training through pain.
- Avoid recording identifiable bystanders; obtain permission before filming or sharing.
- No diagnosis or individualized medical claims; seek a trusted adult/qualified professional for injury or health concerns.

**Project quality rubric**
- target_quality: Target is specific, observable and within learner control.
- measurement: Records are consistent and context-aware.
- practice_design: Drills, dose, progression and recovery are proportionate.
- reasoning: Conclusions acknowledge uncertainty and lead to a justified next step.

**Completion rule:** A defined behaviour has been practised and reviewed across more than one session, with a safe next-step decision and limitations recorded.

**Transfer:** Apply the same observation loop to a different sport skill or non-sport performance.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-003 — Personal Money and Small-Business Record System

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Improve the learner's own understanding of small cash movements and business unit economics using accurate, privacy-conscious records.

**Driving question:** Can I explain where money moved, what a sale actually contributed and what remains uncertain?

**Disciplines:** FIN, MATH, BIZ, WORK, RES, CYBER  
**Target modules:** BASE-FIN-001, BASE-FIN-002, BASE-FIN-003, BASE-FIN-006, BASE-FIN-008, BASE-MATH-004, BASE-WORK-009, BASE-CYBER-001

**Milestones**
1. **Define the decision question:** Choose one low-stakes question such as 'what cash is available?' or 'what does one sale contribute?'.
2. **Create a safe dataset:** Prefer synthetic records; if using real business records, obtain guardian/owner permission and remove names, phone numbers, account IDs and customer details.
3. **Build the ledger:** Record date, category, inflow/outflow, payment status and reference; prevent duplicate entries.
4. **Calculate and reconcile:** Calculate revenue, direct cost, contribution and cash movement separately; check totals against source records.
5. **Explain limitations:** Identify missing costs, refunds, timing, fees and assumptions; do not present a simple model as audited accounts.

**Deliverables**
- A fictional or permissioned transaction dataset
- A transparent ledger with categories and reconciliation
- A unit-economics worksheet separating revenue, direct cost and contribution
- A cash-flow summary and assumptions note
- A privacy and error-check checklist

**Acceptance criteria**
- Revenue, cash balance, cost and profit/contribution are not conflated.
- Transactions reconcile to the supplied dataset and exceptions are visible.
- Private customer and account data are excluded.
- Assumptions and missing costs are explicit.
- No investment, tax or legal recommendation is produced.

**Evidence**
- Data dictionary
- Anonymised/synthetic ledger
- Formula checks and reconciliation
- Exception log
- Short explanation with limitations

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Use an offline spreadsheet or paper ledger.
- low_resource: Use a small synthetic dataset and hand calculations.
- expanded: Add a dashboard only after the ledger and formulas are verified.

**Safety and ethics**
- A minor must not independently handle regulated financial activity, tax filings, contracts or customer payment credentials.
- Real business data require explicit owner/guardian permission and anonymisation.
- No high-risk investing, borrowing, crypto trading or financial promotion.

**Project quality rubric**
- accounting_accuracy: Transactions are consistently classified and totals reconcile.
- quantitative_reasoning: Calculations use correct units and distinguish financial concepts.
- data_protection: Only necessary non-sensitive data are used and stored safely.
- interpretation: The learner explains uncertainty, omitted costs and limits.

**Completion rule:** The learner can reconcile a safe dataset, explain each metric and identify what the data cannot establish.

**Transfer:** Use the same ledger discipline for a separate personal budget or a fictional microbusiness dataset.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-004 — Household Workflow Improvement

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Reduce friction in one low-risk household task through observation, a reversible change and a fair before/after comparison.

**Driving question:** What small change would make one household task easier without creating a new safety or fairness problem?

**Disciplines:** INDEP, WORK, MATH, COMM, LEAD  
**Target modules:** BASE-INDEP-005, BASE-INDEP-006, BASE-WORK-012, BASE-WORK-004, BASE-MATH-005, BASE-COMM-006, BASE-LEAD-002

**Milestones**
1. **Choose a low-risk task:** Select a routine such as organising school materials, laundry sorting or a cleaning checklist; avoid electrical, chemical mixing or structural repairs.
2. **Observe without blaming:** Map the steps, waiting, confusion and handoffs; ask permission before observing others.
3. **Define success and constraints:** Choose one practical measure such as missed items or steps; include safety, accessibility and other household members' needs.
4. **Trial a reversible change:** Use labels, layout, checklist or storage change; do not buy items without approval.
5. **Review and hand over:** Compare outcomes, ask whether the change creates extra work for someone else and document how to maintain or undo it.

**Deliverables**
- A description of the current process
- A simple process map and friction observation
- A proposed reversible change
- A before/after comparison using a modest measure
- A household handoff note and reflection

**Acceptance criteria**
- The selected process is genuinely used and low risk.
- The change is reversible and does not transfer hidden work or risk to others.
- The comparison uses a consistent measure and records context.
- Household members' privacy and preferences are respected.
- The handoff is understandable to the person who will use it.

**Evidence**
- Current-state map
- Consent/permission note where needed
- Proposed change
- Before/after observation
- Handoff and rollback note

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Create a checklist or annotated photo of the learner's own storage area.
- low_resource: Use paper labels or reposition existing items with permission.
- expanded: Compare two alternative layouts or checklists over a short period.

**Safety and ethics**
- Do not mix cleaning chemicals or use hazardous tools.
- Do not alter shared spaces or discard belongings without permission.
- Stop and ask an adult for electrical, gas, plumbing, heavy lifting or repair issues.

**Project quality rubric**
- process_understanding: Accurately represents steps and friction.
- change_quality: Change addresses the cause and is proportionate.
- measurement: Comparison is fair and limitations are noted.
- responsibility: Consent, safety and shared workload are respected.

**Completion rule:** The process improvement is trialled, evaluated and either retained or rolled back with reasons.

**Transfer:** Use the same process map on a different low-risk routine and compare what transfers.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-005 — Bangla–English Personal Technical Glossary

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Solve a real comprehension problem by building a small, concept-accurate bilingual glossary for terms encountered in current study.

**Driving question:** How can I explain the same technical idea accurately in Bangla and English without forcing misleading word-for-word translations?

**Disciplines:** LANG, COMM, RES, MATH, WORK  
**Target modules:** BASE-LANG-009, BASE-LANG-003, BASE-LANG-007, BASE-RES-003, BASE-RES-005, BASE-COMM-005

**Milestones**
1. **Collect real friction points:** Capture terms that caused confusion in school or self-study; avoid padding the list with words already understood.
2. **Find authoritative explanations:** Use objective-matched sources and record provenance; compare more than one explanation for ambiguous terms.
3. **Write concept-first definitions:** Explain the concept in simple language before choosing a translation; add an example and a common misconception.
4. **Test both directions:** Explain from English to Bangla and Bangla to English; ask whether the meaning survives, not whether wording matches.
5. **Revise after retrieval:** Test after a delay, correct misleading entries and mark terms that need context-specific wording.

**Deliverables**
- A list of 20–30 encountered terms
- Concept definitions in plain language in both languages
- Examples, diagrams or counterexamples for difficult terms
- A note on non-equivalent translations and context
- A short self-test and correction log

**Acceptance criteria**
- Entries explain concepts rather than provide bare word substitutions.
- Technical meaning remains stable across both languages.
- Ambiguous terms show context and non-equivalence explicitly.
- Sources can be traced and quotations are attributed.
- Delayed self-test exposes gaps rather than being pre-filled.

**Evidence**
- Term selection rationale
- Source ledger
- Bilingual entries with examples
- Bidirectional test
- Revision log

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Use a notes app or document with a table.
- low_resource: Paper cards with term, concept, example and source.
- expanded: Add audio pronunciation or a small searchable glossary.

**Safety and ethics**
- Do not copy entire textbook passages or copyrighted dictionaries; paraphrase and cite.
- Avoid collecting classmates' private mistakes or personal data.

**Project quality rubric**
- concept_accuracy: Definition and examples are accurate.
- bilingual_fidelity: Meaning is preserved without false equivalence.
- source_quality: Sources and borrowed material are traceable.
- retrieval: Learner can explain terms in both directions after delay.

**Completion rule:** A selected set of difficult terms can be explained accurately in both languages, with uncertainty and context marked.

**Transfer:** Apply the concept-first method to a new science topic or a third language.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PERSONAL-006 — Personal Digital Safety and Backup Drill

**Class:** `personal_use`  
**Goal:** Reduce the learner's own risk of losing important non-sensitive files or compromising accounts through a documented, reversible safety routine.

**Driving question:** Could I recover my important study/project files after a device problem without exposing secrets?

**Disciplines:** CYBER, WORK, ENG, RES, INDEP  
**Target modules:** BASE-CYBER-001, BASE-CYBER-004, BASE-CYBER-005, BASE-CYBER-006, BASE-WORK-001, BASE-WORK-008, BASE-RES-011

**Milestones**
1. **Define what matters:** List categories of non-sensitive learning and project files; do not write passwords, recovery codes or account identifiers into the project.
2. **Organise and protect:** Create predictable folders and filenames; review app permissions and account security settings with a trusted adult if needed.
3. **Choose a backup route:** Use an approved local or removable destination; understand that one copy on the same device is not a reliable backup.
4. **Perform a test restore:** Copy dummy files, verify integrity and restore them to a separate folder; do not wipe or modify the only copy.
5. **Document recovery:** Write steps another person could follow and note what remains unprotected.

**Deliverables**
- A personal data inventory excluding secrets
- A file organisation and naming convention
- A backup plan with at least one independent copy
- A test restore of dummy or non-sensitive files
- A short account/device safety checklist

**Acceptance criteria**
- A test restore succeeds for selected dummy files.
- Backup copy is independent enough to reduce a realistic failure mode.
- No secrets or unnecessary personal data are stored in the artefact.
- The procedure is reversible and avoids destructive commands.
- Limitations and residual risks are documented.

**Evidence**
- Data category inventory
- Folder convention
- Backup diagram
- Test restore record
- Recovery instructions

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Use Android file manager and a safe copy destination; do not assume browser access to SD card.
- low_resource: Use two separate storage locations where available; verify by opening restored files.
- expanded: Add checksums for a small sample and a versioned restore log.

**Safety and ethics**
- Never share passwords, OTPs, recovery phrases or private keys.
- Do not run destructive shell commands or factory reset as part of the project.
- Ask a guardian before changing account recovery or device security settings.

**Project quality rubric**
- risk_model: Identifies realistic assets and failure modes.
- backup_quality: A separate copy is verified by restoration.
- security_hygiene: Secrets and permissions are handled safely.
- reproducibility: Another person could follow the recovery steps.

**Completion rule:** A test restore is completed and the learner can explain which files are protected, where the copy resides and what remains at risk.

**Transfer:** Repeat the restore test on a different file type or device context without exposing account secrets.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PRODUCT-001 — Tiny Digital Product: Problem-to-Prototype Validation

**Class:** `product_business_foundation`  
**Goal:** Build a useful, narrowly scoped digital product prototype around an evidenced user problem and establish a credible path to future improvement, packaging and possible sale without assuming commercial success.

**Driving question:** Can I build a small product that solves a real user task, and what evidence would justify the next investment of effort?

**Disciplines:** ENG, BIZ, RES, COMM, AEST, CYBER, FIN, WORK, LEAD  
**Target modules:** BASE-ENG-001, BASE-ENG-002, BASE-ENG-005, BASE-ENG-007, BASE-BIZ-001, BASE-BIZ-003, BASE-BIZ-004, BASE-RES-001, BASE-RES-003, BASE-RES-007, BASE-COMM-006, BASE-AEST-008, BASE-AEST-009, BASE-CYBER-001, BASE-FIN-008, BASE-WORK-004, BASE-WORK-008

**Milestones**
1. **Discover before building:** Define target user and problem; use consent-based, age-appropriate conversations; separate observed facts from assumptions.
2. **Specify one job:** Write one user story, inputs, outputs, constraints, acceptance criteria and non-goals.
3. **Prototype the smallest complete path:** Build a low-risk local prototype; use dummy data; keep version history and avoid premature architecture.
4. **Test failure and misuse cases:** Test empty, invalid, duplicate and unexpected inputs; review accessibility, privacy and security boundaries.
5. **Model viability without fantasy:** Estimate direct costs, time, support burden and capacity; show scenarios, unknowns and stop conditions.
6. **Decide based on evidence:** Present prototype, results and limitations; select a next experiment or stop. Do not infer product-market fit from a tiny sample.

**Deliverables**
- Problem and user-evidence brief
- One end-to-end prototype for a narrow task
- Acceptance tests and usability observations
- Basic cost/time/capacity model with assumptions
- Privacy/threat checklist using dummy data
- Decision memo: continue, change, pause or stop

**Acceptance criteria**
- The product completes one defined user task end-to-end.
- User/problem evidence is consent-based and appropriately limited.
- Prototype tests include normal and boundary cases.
- Sensitive real user data and unnecessary collection are avoided.
- Costs, time and support burden are stated separately from revenue.
- A stop/change decision is accepted as a valid result.

**Evidence**
- Problem brief and interview notes with identifying data removed
- Versioned prototype
- Test cases/results
- Risk checklist
- Cost/capacity model
- Decision memo

**Scope variants**
- phone_only: Create a clickable or form-based prototype; build locally or use a free tool only if age/account terms permit.
- low_resource: Paper prototype plus manual task test; no hosting or paid services.
- expanded: Build a local app, add packaging documentation and a deployment plan only after prototype evidence.

**Safety and ethics**
- Public release, contracts, paid services, spending, customer accounts and handling real payments require guardian involvement and applicable legal checks.
- Do not collect sensitive personal data, scrape sites, spam, mislead users or test systems without permission.
- Do not promise income or claim product-market fit.

**Project quality rubric**
- problem_evidence: Problem and user claims are traceable and proportionate.
- technical_quality: Prototype works for its defined scope and handles key failures.
- user_quality: Task flow is understandable and accessible.
- responsibility_and_viability: Privacy, cost, capacity and commercial uncertainty are handled honestly.

**Completion rule:** A working or testable prototype, traceable problem evidence, meaningful tests, a realistic cost/capacity view and an evidence-based next decision exist.

**Transfer:** Repeat the discovery and prototype cycle for a different user group; identify which methods transfer and which assumptions do not.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.

### ATH-PROJ-PRODUCT-002 — Small Inventory and Sales Operations Tool

**Class:** `product_business_foundation`  
**Goal:** Create a reliable internal tool or spreadsheet for a small lawful product operation, with reconciled inventory, sales and unit economics.

**Completion rule:** A small, sourced exploration clarifies one linguistic phenomenon and its limits.

**Transfer:** Optional: compare a third language or script if curiosity develops.

**Mastery boundary:** Project completion is not module mastery; assess linked module outcomes separately.
