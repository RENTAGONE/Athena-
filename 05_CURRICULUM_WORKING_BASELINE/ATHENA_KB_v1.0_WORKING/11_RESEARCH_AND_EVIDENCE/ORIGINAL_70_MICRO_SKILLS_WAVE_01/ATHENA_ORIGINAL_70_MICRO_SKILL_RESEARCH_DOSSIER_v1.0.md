## 1. Research interpretation and evidence rules

### What the evidence can support

- For cognitive/learning skills, classroom and cognitive-science syntheses support retrieval practice, spacing, worked examples, explanatory questioning and some interleaving. A 2026 classroom meta-analysis reports average benefits but also meaningful moderators; therefore ATHENA must not hard-code one universal schedule or assume the same effect for every learner and task [SRC-LRN-01–05].
- For motor skills, review-level evidence supports attention, observation, feedback and practice design as relevant variables. A 2025 meta-analysis of self-controlled feedback reports mixed effects across acquisition/retention/transfer; feedback must be tested rather than treated as universally beneficial [SRC-MOT-01–02].
- For communication and creative tasks, the retrieved materials are mostly professional instructional resources. They support practical teaching suggestions, not strong causal claims that a specific protocol reliably produces competence [SRC-COM-01–03, SRC-PHOTO-01–02, SRC-VOICE-01].
- For digital and safety skills, official documentation and public-safety guidance support what learners should recognise or do within scope. They do not prove that reading the material makes a learner secure, medically competent or safe in every context [SRC-DIG-01–04, SRC-SAFE-01–12].

### Evidence labels used in this dossier

- **Direct for task:** source directly specifies the procedure, standard or official documentation relevant to the task.
- **Direct for mechanism / indirect for skill:** research supports the underlying learning method, but not the exact ATHENA micro-skill as a separately validated intervention.
- **Adjacent:** relevant learning principles apply by reasoned transfer; exact task-specific effectiveness remains unestablished.
- **Instructional resource:** useful teaching/reference material, not a controlled efficacy study.
- **Authoritative safety guidance:** supports recognition, escalation or boundaries; does not replace supervised practical training.

### Important calibration rules

1. Do not count exposure, completion, self-report or a polished demonstration as mastery. Require observable performance and delayed retention/transfer evidence.
2. Practice doses and time-to-competence estimates in legacy records are planning hypotheses, not scientifically established prescriptions. Calibrate them with learner trials.
3. Separate acquisition (can do now), retention (can still do later) and transfer (can adapt to a new context).
4. Use a task-specific criterion, not a universal pass percentage. The existing authoring standard explicitly rejects arbitrary universal thresholds.
5. A resource may be authoritative for one claim and irrelevant for another. Keep source-to-claim links narrow.

## 2. Category-level findings

### MS-01 — COGNITIVE / LEARNING
Cognitive learning research supports retrieval, spacing, explanatory questioning, worked examples and integration of representations. This evidence is strongest for retention of taught knowledge; it does not validate every individual cognitive habit as a stand-alone skill.
**Source families:** SRC-LRN-01,SRC-LRN-02,SRC-LRN-03,SRC-LRN-04,SRC-LRN-05.

### MS-02 — DEXTERITY / COORDINATION
Use motor-learning principles: demonstration/observation, attentional focus, manageable repetitions, feedback and delayed retention/transfer checks. Immediate smoothness is not proof of durable learning. Specific evidence for recreational coin/pen manipulation is limited; label protocols as evidence-informed practice rather than clinically validated interventions.
**Source families:** SRC-MOT-01,SRC-MOT-02.

### MS-03 — PRACTICAL / EVERYDAY
Teach a safe, repeatable sequence with a visible quality check and realistic materials. Use authoritative safety or measurement references where relevant. For ordinary household routines, the practice sequence is a pragmatic instructional design, not a claim of experimentally validated training dose.
**Source families:** SRC-SAFE-05,SRC-MEAS-01,SRC-ROUTE-01.

### MS-04 — SOCIAL / COMMUNICATION
Use observable behaviours, realistic role-play, listener feedback and transfer to a new partner/context. Communication resources here are professional instructional guides, not proof that one script works universally. Avoid equating eye contact or a particular communication style with honesty, attention or competence; accommodate culture, disability and neurodiversity.
**Source families:** SRC-COM-01,SRC-COM-02.

### MS-05 — DIGITAL / TECHNICAL
Use authentic tasks in a sandbox or non-critical context, explicit verification and recovery steps. Security guidance should favour unique passwords/passphrases, password managers, MFA, phishing resistance and verified backups. Never practise on accounts, systems or data without authorization.
**Source families:** SRC-DIG-01,SRC-DIG-02,SRC-DIG-03,SRC-DIG-04.

### MS-06 — VISUAL / CREATIVE
Teach one visual variable at a time, compare examples, make deliberate variations and evaluate against the intended visual outcome. Phone photography references are instructional rather than controlled learning research. Pitch/rhythm and breathing need separate auditory/vocal sources; avoid forcing voice or breath.
**Source families:** SRC-PHOTO-01,SRC-PHOTO-02,SRC-VOICE-01,SRC-MOT-01.

### MS-07 — SAFETY / EMERGENCY LITERACY
The learner outcome is recognition, prevention, communication and escalation—not unsupervised rescue, medical treatment, electrical repair or firefighting. Use local emergency information and qualified providers. For Bangladesh, the Police source lists 999; verify it on a recurring basis. CPR/AED and choking entries remain course-readiness pathways only.
**Source families:** SRC-SAFE-01,SRC-SAFE-02,SRC-SAFE-03,SRC-SAFE-04,SRC-SAFE-06,SRC-SAFE-07,SRC-SAFE-08,SRC-SAFE-09,SRC-SAFE-10,SRC-SAFE-11,SRC-SAFE-12.

**Sources:** SRC-LRN-01, SRC-LRN-02, SRC-LRN-03, SRC-LRN-05

## 4. Cross-cutting upgrades required before approval

### 4.1 Protocol design

- Each protocol should state the exact task boundary, starting condition, materials, environment, observable steps, common errors, stop conditions, feedback method, and what constitutes safe completion.
- Add a minimum viable practice dose and an optional extension only where the task supports it. Do not force a second curriculum of micro-skills onto the learner.
- Use demonstration/observation for motor skills, but require unaided performance after the demonstration is removed.
- Include a no-feedback or delayed-feedback check where appropriate; immediate correction can improve a practice attempt without proving durable learning.
- For interpersonal skills, evaluate whether the other person understood and consented; do not score eye contact, extroversion, accent or culturally specific body language as competence.

### 4.2 Assessment and evidence capture

- Store task version, date, context, aids permitted, number of trials, observed errors, feedback given, result, assessor identity/role and delayed recheck.
- Use at least one novel transfer condition for skills whose purpose is generalisation.
- Use an independent observer for safety-critical and interpersonal assessments where feasible; self-rating alone is insufficient.
- Do not collect recordings or personal communication samples without consent and data minimisation.

### 4.3 Scheduling and retention

- Schedule according to relevance, performance decay and risk, not one universal D1/D2/D3/D4 code treated as a scientific interval.
- For knowledge-heavy tasks, use spaced retrieval with feedback; choose intervals based on intended retention and observed performance [SRC-LRN-01, SRC-LRN-03, SRC-LRN-05].
- For physical tasks, use short high-quality practice bouts, rest, varied contexts after stable basics and delayed retention tests; avoid repetitive practice through pain [SRC-MOT-01–02].
- For safety literacy, re-check official local numbers, policies and training-provider details periodically.

### 4.4 Risk and safeguarding

- `MS-07-009` and `MS-07-010` remain course-readiness pathways only. They must never become text-only CPR/choking instructions or award competence.
- `MS-07-007` is a decision/evacuation literacy item; the USFA says young children should not use extinguishers. For young learners, the default action is alert, evacuate and call for help [SRC-SAFE-07, SRC-SAFE-12].
- `MS-07-005` is hazard recognition and escalation only. Never ask a learner to touch, unplug, open or repair suspect electrical equipment [SRC-SAFE-04].
- `MS-03-003` and `MS-03-007` need age-appropriate supervision and a strict boundary around needles/tools [SRC-SAFE-05].
- `MS-03-010` and `MS-07-001` need local-context updates; the Bangladesh Police site lists 999 as emergency service, but ATHENA must verify live local details before deployment [SRC-SAFE-02–03].
