# ATHENA Knowledge Base v1.0 — Build Manifest

**Status:** Working build; not a release candidate.  
**Canonical parent:** ATHENA Master Vault v4.8 working + Knowledge Architecture Pack v0.9 working.  
**Release rule:** Do not label or publish v1.0 until the completeness gates in this document pass.

## Release objective

Deliver a populated, validated, importable first deployment baseline for ATHENA. It must provide real learning pathways—not only taxonomy—for all 18 disciplines, a usable cross-disciplinary foundations curriculum, assessment/evidence alignment, retention and transfer protocols, curated resource records, and integrated projects.

## Required content layers

1. **Taxonomy:** discipline → domain → skill family → skill → subskill; concepts and contexts linked where appropriate.
2. **Instruction:** learning units with prerequisite knowledge, concise explanations, worked examples, misconceptions, guided practice, independent practice, and application.
3. **Objectives:** observable, condition-specific outcomes with explicit success criteria.
4. **Assessment:** retrieval checks, procedural performance, explanation, authentic tasks, transfer tasks, and evidence rules.
5. **Retention:** item-type-specific review and maintenance profiles; relearning preserves historical evidence.
6. **Transfer:** explicit cross-domain links with mechanism, distance, expected benefit, and limitations.
7. **Resources:** source provenance, access/verification date, license/usage notes where known, and quality limitations. Never fabricate a source or URL.
8. **Projects:** integrated outputs that require knowledge from multiple disciplines and produce inspectable evidence.
9. **Importability:** stable IDs, schema-valid records, resolvable references, duplicate/conflict checks, graph checks, and atomic preview/commit compatibility.

## Coverage gates for v1.0

- All 18 disciplines have reviewed domain maps, meaningful skill-family trees, and six-stage progression coverage.
- Every active foundational pathway has complete units, objectives, practice, assessments, evidence criteria, and retention rules.
- Every active discipline has at least one complete entry pathway and a defensible bridge to intermediate study; advanced tiers are mapped with explicit entry competencies and real content rather than empty labels.
- No objective is orphaned from instruction and assessment; no assessment claims competence without observable evidence.
- All hard prerequisites are justified and the hard-prerequisite graph is acyclic.
- All cross-references resolve; stable IDs are unique and immutable.
- Content status and review status are honest. Draft content is not presented as expert-approved.
- Import validation passes against the canonical schemas and exchange mapping; a test import/rollback is completed in the application before deployment is called ready.

## Build sequence

A. Canonical foundations and diagnostic placement.  
B. Mathematics, logic, learning science, research literacy, language/communication, digital workflow, scientific reasoning, and project execution pathways.  
C. Full discipline trees and entry-to-intermediate learning units.  
D. Advanced/specialist pathways, including programming-language and second-language tracks where justified by capability goals.  
E. Assessment banks, practical protocols, resources, integrated projects, and transfer graph.  
F. Schema validation, graph validation, content audit, import test, and release packaging.

## Non-goals / anti-patterns

No XP-as-competence, streak punishment, leaderboard, fake mastery percentage, forced linear progression, fabricated resources, generic placeholder units, or one universal review interval. A user may explore content even where a pedagogical prerequisite is unmet; only justified hard prerequisites may gate performance or progression.
