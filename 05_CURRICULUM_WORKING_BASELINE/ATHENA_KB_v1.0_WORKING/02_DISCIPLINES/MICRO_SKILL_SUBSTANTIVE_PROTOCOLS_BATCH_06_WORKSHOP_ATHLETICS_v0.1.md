# ATHENA Substantive Micro-skill Protocols — Batch 06

**Status:** Substantive authored drafts; not independently reviewed, calibrated or certified.

**Scope:** 20 workshop and athletic-movement protocols. All tool/electronics activities are limited to supervised, isolated, low-voltage practice; athletic drills are low-intensity foundations, not medical or performance guarantees.


## MS-09-001 — Select the correct screwdriver bit

**Target performance:** Match a replaceable driver bit to the fastener recess and size, then verify full seating before applying force.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a nonpowered hand driver and loose practice fasteners on a stable bench. Identify bit markings where present.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Inspect the screw head and identify the drive family. 2. Compare candidate bits for shape and size; the correct bit should seat deeply with minimal rocking. 3. Check that the bit is not rounded, chipped or contaminated. 4. Hold the driver in line with the screw axis and test a gentle hand turn. 5. Stop if the bit cams out or the recess deforms. 6. Try a correctly sized alternative rather than adding force. 7. Return the fastener to its starting state and label the matching bit.

**Practice dose:** Match five fasteners to bits; one controlled attempt per pairing, then explain any uncertain match.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least four of five pairings seat correctly and turn without slipping or damaging the head.

**Common failure modes:** Choosing by colour alone; confusing Phillips and Pozidriv; using worn bits; compensating for poor fit with force.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, identify unfamiliar drive types from a sample and select a bit before turning.

**Transfer task:** Explain why a correct fit matters more than driver size or handle leverage.

**Safety and scope:** No powered tools or live equipment. Keep bits away from children and eyes; use adult supervision for repairs.

**Evidence to record:** Fastener type, bit marking/size, seating stability, slip observations and delayed matching.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-002 — Use a hand tool with controlled force

**Target performance:** Apply only the force needed for a simple hand-tool task while maintaining stable grip, alignment and a safe work position.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a blunt, low-risk hand tool and a practice material approved by an adult. Secure the workpiece; do not use damaged tools.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the tool’s intended use and inspect handle, head and working edge. 2. Position the workpiece so it cannot roll or slide. 3. Choose a grip that keeps fingers outside the tool’s path. 4. Align the tool with the task before applying force. 5. Begin with light pressure and increase only enough to achieve the intended result. 6. Stop if the tool slips, material behaves unexpectedly or more force seems necessary than expected. 7. Reposition the workpiece or choose a suitable tool rather than forcing it. 8. Put the tool down safely after use.

**Practice dose:** Practise a single controlled task for 5–10 minutes under supervision; no speed challenge.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete the task without tool slip, damage or loss of control and explain when to stop or change tools.

**Common failure modes:** Using body weight instead of control; holding work by hand near the tool path; using a tool for an unintended purpose; continuing after a slip.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat a comparable task after several days and check whether force remains controlled.

**Transfer task:** Compare two tools designed for similar but distinct tasks and justify the safer choice.

**Safety and scope:** Adult supervision required. No blades, high-force tasks, power tools, mains equipment or improvised leverage. Wear task-appropriate protection.

**Evidence to record:** Tool inspection, workpiece stability, task outcome, slips/stops and supervisor feedback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-003 — Clamp a workpiece securely

**Target performance:** Position a suitable clamp so a practice workpiece remains stable without crushing or distorting it.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a small hand clamp and soft practice material on a stable bench. No cutting or drilling during this protocol.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Inspect the clamp for cracks, damaged threads and smooth movement. 2. Select a clamp size and pad type appropriate to the workpiece. 3. Place the workpiece on a stable surface and identify where pressure can be applied safely. 4. Add protective pads if the material can be marked. 5. Tighten gradually until the workpiece no longer shifts under gentle hand pressure. 6. Check that the clamp does not bend or crush the material. 7. Recheck stability before any separate supervised operation. 8. Release slowly and store the clamp.

**Practice dose:** Secure three different safe materials, adjusting pressure rather than using maximum force.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Workpiece does not shift during a gentle stability check and shows no crushing, cracking or clamp damage.

**Common failure modes:** Overtightening; clamping on a fragile edge; using an undersized clamp; placing fingers in pinch points.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, select a clamp and pad for a new practice material and explain the pressure choice.

**Transfer task:** Explain why workholding is a prerequisite to many workshop operations but does not itself make a cutting task safe.

**Safety and scope:** Pinch hazard. Adult supervision; no cutting/drilling/sawing as part of this protocol; keep hands clear of moving jaws.

**Evidence to record:** Clamp type, material, pad use, stability test, damage inspection and delayed selection.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-004 — Measure and mark a straight cut line

**Target performance:** Transfer a measured dimension to a workpiece and mark a visible straight reference line without performing the cut.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use scrap cardboard or soft practice material, ruler/tape and pencil. No knife or saw.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Confirm the required dimension and the edge from which it is measured. 2. Check that the reference edge is straight and stable. 3. Measure from the correct zero point and mark two points separated along the intended line. 4. Align a straightedge through both marks. 5. Draw a fine line on the correct side of the measurement, accounting for any stated material allowance only if instructed. 6. Re-measure from the original edge to verify the line. 7. Label the waste side if the task requires it. 8. Ask a second person to check before any later cutting operation.

**Practice dose:** Make three measured marks on scrap material; vary the dimension but keep the method consistent.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Each line is straight and within 2 mm of the target at both ends; learner can identify which side represents the retained material.

**Common failure modes:** Measuring from a damaged edge; marking only one point; thick pencil line obscuring the reference; forgetting cut width/kerf when a real cut is later planned.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, mark a different dimension and verify it independently.

**Transfer task:** Explain the difference between a reference line and the actual cut path, and why material removal allowance may matter.

**Safety and scope:** This protocol ends at marking. Cutting requires separate training, correct tools, workholding, PPE and adult supervision.

**Evidence to record:** Target dimension, tool resolution, measured endpoints, line deviation and independent check.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-005 — Identify common fastener types

**Target performance:** Distinguish common screws, bolts, nuts, washers and anchors by visible features and describe their typical role without assuming interchangeability.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a labelled sample tray of blunt, ordinary hardware; avoid sharp, corroded or unknown items.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Separate threaded fasteners from unthreaded hardware. 2. Identify screws by their head/drive and typical direct engagement with a material. 3. Identify bolts by their intended use with a nut or threaded component, noting that naming conventions vary. 4. Identify nuts by internal threads and washers by their load-distribution or spacing role. 5. Distinguish wall anchors/plugs from fasteners themselves. 6. Compare thread diameter, pitch, length and head style. 7. Use labels or a reference chart to confirm uncertain types. 8. Record that material and load rating matter in real applications.

**Practice dose:** Sort 15 sample items into types and explain the role of five; use a reference chart.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly classify at least 12 of 15 items and avoid claiming a load rating from appearance alone.

**Common failure modes:** Assuming all screws are interchangeable; confusing a washer with a spacer; selecting by length only; inferring strength from size or colour.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, classify a fresh sample and state what information is needed before selecting it for a real load.

**Transfer task:** Use a hardware catalogue to choose a fastener for a hypothetical project, noting material compatibility and manufacturer specifications.

**Safety and scope:** Do not use unknown fasteners in structural, overhead, electrical or safety-critical applications. Ask a qualified adult/professional.

**Evidence to record:** Sample IDs, classification, reference source, uncertain cases and delayed result.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-006 — Inspect a tool before use

**Target performance:** Perform a repeatable pre-use inspection and remove damaged or unsuitable tools from service.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use clean, unplugged hand tools in a supervised workspace. Do not inspect live or powered equipment internally.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Confirm the tool is appropriate for the intended task. 2. Check handle, grip, head, shaft and working surfaces for cracks, looseness, corrosion or deformation. 3. Check moving parts for binding or excessive play. 4. Confirm guards and safety features are present where applicable. 5. Check that the tool is clean and dry enough to grip safely. 6. Compare with manufacturer guidance if damage is uncertain. 7. Tag or set aside a suspect tool and tell the responsible adult; do not attempt an improvised repair. 8. Return serviceable tools to a designated location.

**Practice dose:** Inspect five different unplugged tools using the same checklist; no dismantling.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Identify all deliberately introduced visible defects in a supervised training set and correctly remove suspect tools from use.

**Common failure modes:** Testing a damaged tool under load; ignoring loose handles; removing guards; assuming a tool is safe because it worked last time.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat inspection before a later task and explain whether storage conditions changed tool condition.

**Transfer task:** Create a pre-use checklist for a small electronics or craft station and distinguish user inspection from formal maintenance.

**Safety and scope:** Do not inspect inside mains-powered equipment or batteries. Damaged tools must be repaired/replaced by a competent person.

**Evidence to record:** Tool, inspection checklist, defects, disposition and supervisor confirmation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-007 — Strip a wire to a specified length

**Target performance:** Prepare the end of an isolated low-voltage practice wire by removing a controlled insulation length without nicking the conductor.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use only scrap low-voltage wire disconnected from every power source, a correctly sized wire stripper and adult supervision. This is not mains wiring training.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Confirm the wire is completely disconnected and identify conductor size/type. 2. Select the stripper notch that matches the wire gauge using markings or a reference chart. 3. Set the target strip length and mark it on the insulation. 4. Position the tool at the mark without twisting or crushing the conductor. 5. Close the tool and remove the insulation with a controlled motion. 6. Inspect all sides for nicked, cut or missing strands. 7. Compare exposed conductor length with the target. 8. Discard damaged practice ends and restart with fresh scrap wire.

**Practice dose:** Practise on short pieces of scrap wire under direct supervision; no connection to a circuit.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Three consecutive ends fall within ±1 mm of target and show no visible conductor nicking or strand loss.

**Common failure modes:** Using the wrong gauge notch; stripping live wire; cutting strands; leaving too much bare conductor; pulling at an angle.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat on a different scrap wire size only after confirming tool compatibility.

**Transfer task:** Explain why damaged strands and excessive exposed conductor can create reliability or short-circuit hazards.

**Safety and scope:** Never strip mains or connected wire. Adult supervision required; keep wire cutters away from skin and eyes. Real wiring requires qualified instruction and applicable standards.

**Evidence to record:** Wire type/gauge, target length, measured length, conductor inspection and supervisor sign-off.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-008 — Make a secure low-voltage breadboard connection

**Target performance:** Build a simple, unpowered breadboard connection with components seated correctly and no unintended short between rows.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a breadboard, low-voltage battery pack or current-limited educational supply kept disconnected during assembly, resistors and jumper wires. Adult supervision.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the breadboard terminal strips and power rails from its actual layout; do not assume every board connects rails identically. 2. Inspect for bent contacts and damaged wires. 3. Keep the supply disconnected while placing components. 4. Insert each lead fully into the intended row without forcing it. 5. Trace the intended electrical path on paper and compare every connection to the layout. 6. Check polarity for any polarised component before power is considered. 7. Ask a supervisor to inspect the circuit; use a multimeter continuity check only if trained. 8. Power only under supervision with a suitable current-limited low-voltage source and correct protection.

**Practice dose:** Build one simple resistor-only demonstration with no power initially; make and inspect three deliberate layout variations.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Connections match the paper circuit; components are not bent/damaged; no unintended row/rail connection is present; supervisor approves before any power.

**Common failure modes:** Assuming all breadboard rails are continuous; placing both leads in the same connected row; powering while wiring; forcing oversized leads.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, reproduce the unpowered layout from a diagram and explain the board’s internal connectivity.

**Transfer task:** Transfer to a simple LED-resistor circuit only after separate polarity/current-limiting instruction and adult review.

**Safety and scope:** No mains voltage, high-current batteries, damaged cells or unattended power. Disconnect supply before changes. Adult supervision mandatory.

**Evidence to record:** Breadboard model/layout, circuit sketch, component list, continuity/check results and supervisor review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-009 — Identify resistor colour bands

**Target performance:** Read a resistor colour code using a reliable chart and verify the result against tolerance and measurement where appropriate.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use ordinary through-hole resistors, a current code chart and a multimeter only if trained. Do not connect to a powered circuit.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the number of bands and the likely reading direction from the tolerance band spacing/colour. 2. Use the correct chart for the resistor code system. 3. Translate significant-digit bands and multiplier into resistance. 4. Read the tolerance band and express the allowed range. 5. Check whether the colour can be distinguished under current lighting; use a second light source rather than guessing. 6. Compare with a multimeter measurement only when the resistor is isolated and the learner knows the meter procedure. 7. Record uncertainty for faded or ambiguous bands.

**Practice dose:** Decode ten labelled practice resistors; include four-, five-band and tolerance variations if available.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly decode at least 8 of 10 values and tolerance bands; calculations are shown and ambiguous colours are flagged.

**Common failure modes:** Reading from wrong end; confusing multiplier with significant digit; treating measured in-circuit resistance as exact; ignoring tolerance.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, decode five unfamiliar resistors without the chart first, then verify.

**Transfer task:** Use decoded value to select a component for a hypothetical low-voltage circuit, checking power rating and design calculations separately.

**Safety and scope:** Do not measure powered circuits. Avoid shorting battery terminals; use a qualified adult for circuit design and powered tests.

**Evidence to record:** Resistor code, chart version, calculated value, tolerance range, meter result if safely obtained and uncertainty.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-09-010 — Keep a tidy tool and parts station

**Target performance:** Maintain a clear, labelled workspace that reduces lost components, contamination and accidental damage.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a small supervised practice station with a limited set of hand tools and electronic components.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Define zones for active work, tools, components, waste and finished items. 2. Label containers by type/value rather than relying only on colour. 3. Keep small parts in closed trays and sharp tools in a designated safe location. 4. Keep liquids, food and unrelated devices away from electronics. 5. During work, return unused parts to their labelled container and keep only current-task items on the mat. 6. At the end, count or visually check borrowed tools and parts. 7. Remove waste safely and leave the surface clean and dry. 8. Photograph or diagram the layout if it must be recreated.

**Practice dose:** Set up and close down a small station before and after two short practice sessions.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** All tools and components are accounted for; the next session can begin without searching; no loose sharp items or mixed components remain.

**Common failure modes:** Over-organising into too many labels; leaving live circuits powered; mixing resistor values; hiding damaged parts in storage.

**Delayed retention check:** Recreate the station after a week using the labels and compare retrieval time.

**Transfer task:** Apply the same zones to a craft, repair or study workspace and adjust for its specific hazards.

**Safety and scope:** Disconnect power before tidying circuits. Store sharp tools, batteries and small parts safely; supervise around younger children and pets.

**Evidence to record:** Station layout, inventory/checklist, missing items, setup/close time and safety observations.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-001 — Perform a balanced athletic landing

**Target performance:** Land from a low, controlled hop with quiet contact, stable alignment and no loss of balance.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a flat, non-slip surface, supportive footwear and a pain-free warm-up. Begin with very low hops; no boxes or height challenges.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Stand with feet about hip-width and knees soft. 2. Perform a small vertical hop, only a few centimetres. 3. Land on both feet with knees tracking in line with toes and hips bending to absorb force. 4. Keep trunk controlled and weight distributed across the foot. 5. Hold the landing for two seconds without extra steps. 6. Repeat from a slightly different stance only if the first version is stable. 7. Stop if pain, instability or repeated noisy/rigid landings occur.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of 4–5 low hops with full rest; no fatigue challenge.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least 8 of 10 landings are controlled, quiet and held for two seconds with no pain or obvious knee collapse.

**Common failure modes:** Landing stiff-legged; knees collapsing inward; excessive jump height; continuing after pain; training on slippery ground.

**Delayed retention check:** Recheck after 48–72 hours and compare control, not jump height.

**Transfer task:** Apply the same absorption concept to a low-intensity sport movement under coach/qualified supervision.

**Safety and scope:** Not rehabilitation or injury diagnosis. Stop for pain, dizziness or instability; consult a qualified professional for persistent symptoms or prior injury.

**Evidence to record:** Surface, footwear, repetitions, stable-land count, pain/comfort and delayed comparison.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-002 — Accelerate over a short distance

**Target performance:** Build speed progressively over a short, clear distance using coordinated forward drive without maximal sprinting.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a flat, dry, unobstructed surface with safe run-off. Warm up and use submaximal effort.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Start from a balanced standing position. 2. Lean slightly from the ankles as movement begins; avoid folding at the waist. 3. Push the ground backward with alternating legs while arms move in opposition. 4. Increase stride length naturally as speed builds rather than reaching far ahead. 5. Run only 10–15 metres at approximately 70–80% effort. 6. Decelerate gradually in the clear run-off area. 7. Rest fully before another repetition and stop if technique deteriorates.

**Practice dose:** Three to four short accelerations with 60–90 seconds or more rest; do not add volume when fatigued.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Maintain balance and coordinated arm-leg action across repetitions, with gradual acceleration and controlled deceleration.

**Common failure modes:** All-out sprinting; overstriding; leaning from waist; stopping abruptly; repeating while tired.

**Delayed retention check:** After 3–5 days, repeat a small number of submaximal runs and compare movement quality, not maximum time.

**Transfer task:** Use in football warm-up only after a proper warm-up and coach guidance; progress distance and intensity gradually.

**Safety and scope:** No sprinting on wet, crowded or uneven ground. Stop for pain, unusual breathlessness or dizziness. Growth, sleep and recovery matter; no maximal testing is required.

**Evidence to record:** Surface, warm-up, distance, perceived effort, rest and movement-quality notes.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-003 — Decelerate under control

**Target performance:** Reduce running speed through progressive steps and body positioning without abrupt braking or loss of balance.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a clear, flat surface and begin from a gentle jog, not a sprint.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Jog at comfortable speed toward a marked stopping zone. 2. Lower the centre of mass slightly as the stop begins. 3. Use several short, controlled steps rather than one locked-knee brake. 4. Keep trunk balanced and knees aligned with feet. 5. Finish inside the marked zone and hold balance. 6. Repeat from a slightly faster jog only if control remains good. 7. Practise both forward and gentle angled deceleration under supervision.

**Practice dose:** Three to five repetitions from jog pace with full recovery; no sudden maximal stops.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Stop within the target zone on at least four of five attempts without slipping, knee collapse or extra recovery steps.

**Common failure modes:** Trying to stop instantly; locking knees; leaning backward; practising on loose or slippery ground.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat from the same jog pace and compare stopping control.

**Transfer task:** Apply to receiving a football pass or changing direction only after basic deceleration is stable.

**Safety and scope:** Avoid high-speed cutting drills without coaching. Stop if joint pain, slipping or instability occurs.

**Evidence to record:** Surface, approach pace, stopping distance, balance, repetitions and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-004 — Change direction with body control

**Target performance:** Perform a low-speed planned direction change while maintaining balance, controlled foot placement and knee alignment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a flat open area with two visible markers set several metres apart. Begin at walking/jogging pace.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Approach the marker at low speed. 2. Shorten steps before the turn rather than planting suddenly at full speed. 3. Lower the body slightly and keep hips/trunk controlled. 4. Place the outside foot securely and turn the body toward the new direction. 5. Push away smoothly and accelerate only after balance is regained. 6. Repeat in both directions. 7. Increase speed only over later sessions and with coaching if technique is consistent.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of three turns each direction at low speed; rest between sets.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete at least five of six turns each way without slipping, uncontrolled trunk rotation or obvious knee collapse.

**Common failure modes:** Planting a straight leg at high speed; turning only the shoulders; crossing feet awkwardly; increasing speed too early.

**Delayed retention check:** After 3–5 days, repeat at the same speed and compare control on both sides.

**Transfer task:** Use the skill in a simple football cone drill under coach guidance, prioritising control over time.

**Safety and scope:** No high-speed cutting on wet/uneven ground. Stop for pain or instability; prior injury requires qualified advice.

**Evidence to record:** Marker spacing, direction, speed level, stable turns and any discomfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-005 — Receive and cushion a football pass

**Target performance:** Receive a ground pass by adjusting body position and using a suitable contact surface to reduce ball speed and keep it controllable.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a suitable ball, flat safe surface and short passing distance with a partner.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Check surroundings and orient the body so the next action is visible. 2. Move into the ball’s path rather than waiting rigidly. 3. Choose the inside of the foot for a basic ground pass. 4. Withdraw the foot slightly at contact to cushion the ball rather than block it stiffly. 5. Keep the first touch within a comfortable playing distance. 6. Adjust body angle so the ball moves toward the intended next space. 7. Repeat with both feet at low speed before increasing pass pace.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of 8–10 passes per foot with short rest; use a manageable distance.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least 7 of 10 receptions stay within one comfortable step and the learner remains balanced.

**Common failure modes:** Stiff foot; standing square; watching only the ball and not scanning; first touch too far away; forcing weaker foot at high pace.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat after several days with a partner and compare control at the same distance.

**Transfer task:** Receive then pass to a marked target in two touches, adding a simple decision after basic control is reliable.

**Safety and scope:** Warm up; use a safe area away from roads/windows; stop for pain. Avoid excessive repetition when fatigued.

**Evidence to record:** Pass distance/speed, foot used, controllable receptions and first-touch distance estimate.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-006 — Pass accurately to a moving target

**Target performance:** Deliver a controlled pass into the path of a moving partner or target, adjusting direction and pace to the receiver’s movement.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a safe field or open space, ball and cooperative partner walking/jogging slowly.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Observe the partner’s direction and speed before passing. 2. Choose a target point slightly ahead of the receiver, not directly at their current position. 3. Set the non-kicking foot beside the ball and point it toward the intended path. 4. Strike with a controlled surface and moderate pace. 5. Watch whether the ball reaches the receiver’s path at a usable speed. 6. Adjust lead distance or force on the next attempt, changing one variable at a time. 7. Practise both directions and include a stationary target as a baseline.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of six passes at walking pace; progress to light jogging only when accuracy is stable.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least 8 of 12 passes reach a usable receiving zone without requiring the partner to stop abruptly or stretch dangerously.

**Common failure modes:** Passing to where the receiver was; overhitting; ignoring surface/ball condition; changing aim and power simultaneously.

**Delayed retention check:** After 3–5 days, repeat with a different movement direction and compare usable-pass rate.

**Transfer task:** Use in a small-sided football drill, adding a defender only under coach supervision and after control is established.

**Safety and scope:** No hard passes toward people at close range. Use a safe area and suitable footwear; stop for pain or unsafe surface conditions.

**Evidence to record:** Target movement, distance, pass surface, usable-pass count and adjustment notes.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-007 — Scan before receiving a ball

**Target performance:** Gather relevant visual information before the ball arrives so the first touch and next action are better informed.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a partner passing slowly in an open area with one or two visible target zones; no defenders initially.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Before the pass, glance over one shoulder to locate the target space or partner. 2. Reorient eyes to the ball as it travels. 3. Take another brief look if time allows, without losing ball tracking. 4. Receive with body orientation that keeps the next option available. 5. State the target or next action before the pass in early practice. 6. Repeat with a different target position. 7. Add a passive visual cue only after the scanning pattern is consistent.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of six passes; keep pace slow enough to scan without rushing.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** On at least 8 of 10 attempts, learner identifies the target before or as the ball arrives and maintains a controllable first touch.

**Common failure modes:** Looking away too long; scanning after the ball has arrived; turning blindly; treating head movement as useful scanning without extracting information.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, repeat with an unfamiliar target arrangement and compare decision accuracy.

**Transfer task:** Transfer to a small-sided game by scanning for space and teammate positions before the pass, under coach supervision.

**Safety and scope:** Use safe open space and low pass speed. Avoid drills that create collisions; stop if dizzy or uncomfortable.

**Evidence to record:** Scans completed, information identified, first-touch control and next-action choice.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-008 — Use the weaker foot for a controlled pass

**Target performance:** Execute a short, low-force pass with the less-practised foot while maintaining balance and a predictable contact surface.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a stationary ball and close target on a flat surface. Warm up both sides; do not begin with powerful kicks.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Set the non-kicking foot beside the ball at a comfortable distance. 2. Keep ankle firm but not rigid and use the inside of the foot. 3. Make a small backswing and pass gently toward a wide target. 4. Focus on contact and direction rather than power. 5. Adjust stance one variable at a time. 6. Alternate a few dominant-foot passes to compare technique without treating sides as identical. 7. Increase distance only when short passes are repeatable.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of 6–8 gentle passes, with rest; stop before fatigue degrades control.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least 6 of 8 short passes reach a wide target with balanced follow-through and no pain.

**Common failure modes:** Trying to match dominant-foot power; leaning away; swinging too hard; practising only when fatigued; comparing progress unrealistically.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat the same distance and target and compare accuracy, not speed.

**Transfer task:** Use weaker foot in a cooperative passing drill before adding defenders or shooting.

**Safety and scope:** No maximal kicking or painful repetition. Warm up and use a safe surface; persistent pain requires rest and qualified advice.

**Evidence to record:** Foot, distance, target width, accurate-pass count, balance and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-009 — Maintain a steady aerobic pace

**Target performance:** Choose and sustain a conversational exercise intensity for a planned easy session without turning it into a race.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a safe familiar route or open space, suitable footwear and a trusted adult’s awareness. Account for heat, hydration and recovery.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Begin with 5–10 minutes of easy warm-up walking and mobility. 2. Start at a pace where short sentences remain comfortable. 3. Check breathing and perceived effort every few minutes. 4. Slow down or walk if speech becomes difficult, form deteriorates or conditions are hot. 5. Keep the route and duration modest and familiar. 6. Cool down with easy walking. 7. Record how the session felt and whether recovery was normal.

**Practice dose:** Start with 10–20 minutes total easy movement, adjusting to current fitness; allow rest days and avoid increasing duration and intensity together.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete the planned easy session while maintaining conversational effort and normal recovery; no time or distance record is required.

**Common failure modes:** Starting too fast; treating every session as a test; ignoring heat or fatigue; increasing volume abruptly; training through illness.

**Delayed retention check:** Compare perceived effort and recovery after several days, not speed. Adjust future sessions conservatively.

**Transfer task:** Use the pace model for an easy football conditioning day, keeping intense intervals separate and coach-guided.

**Safety and scope:** Stop for chest pain, faintness, unusual breathlessness, heat illness symptoms or sharp pain and seek adult/medical help. Hydrate and avoid extreme heat.

**Evidence to record:** Duration, route, conditions, conversational effort, perceived exertion and recovery.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-10-010 — Perform a safe dynamic warm-up sequence

**Target performance:** Prepare for light-to-moderate activity with progressive movement that increases readiness without fatigue or painful stretching.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a clear surface and activity-appropriate footwear. Select movements within comfortable range; adapt for limitations.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Begin with 2–3 minutes of easy walking or marching. 2. Add controlled joint movements such as ankle rolls and shoulder circles without forcing end range. 3. Perform dynamic leg swings only within comfortable range while holding stable support if needed. 4. Add gentle walking lunges or step-backs only if pain-free and technically controlled. 5. Include activity-specific low-intensity movement such as easy passing or short skips. 6. Increase pace gradually. 7. Finish feeling warmer and more prepared, not tired.

**Practice dose:** Use 5–10 minutes before activity; tailor to sport, weather and individual needs.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner completes the sequence without pain, loss of balance or fatigue and can explain why intensity progresses gradually.

**Common failure modes:** Static bouncing at end range; copying advanced drills; rushing from cold to maximal sprint; using warm-up to exhaust oneself.

**Delayed retention check:** Review after several sessions and remove any movement that repeatedly causes discomfort.

**Transfer task:** Create a sport-specific version that progresses from general movement to the first activity at low intensity.

**Safety and scope:** Warm-up is not injury-proofing. Stop for pain, dizziness or illness; adapt with coach/qualified guidance, especially after injury.

**Evidence to record:** Sequence, duration, movements selected, comfort, readiness and modifications.
