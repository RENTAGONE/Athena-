# ATHENA Substantive Micro-skill Protocols — Batch 07

**Status:** Substantive authored drafts; not independently reviewed, calibrated or certified.

**Scope:** 20 phone-photography and vocal/audio protocols. These are foundational practice guides, not professional photography, singing or clinical voice training.


## MS-11-001 — Clean a phone camera lens safely

**Target performance:** Remove fingerprints and dust from a phone camera lens without scratching the cover glass or introducing liquid into openings.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a clean microfiber cloth and a phone that is not charging. Follow device-maker guidance for cleaning.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Inspect the camera cover in good light without touching it. 2. Check the cloth for grit or debris and use a clean section. 3. Wipe gently with a dry microfiber cloth using light pressure. 4. If residue remains, follow manufacturer guidance for a minimally damp cloth; never spray liquid directly on the phone. 5. Keep moisture away from ports, seams and microphones. 6. Reinspect the lens and take a test photo of a detailed subject. 7. Compare haze or flare before and after cleaning.

**Practice dose:** Clean only when needed; one gentle pass followed by a test image is preferable to repeated rubbing.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** No visible residue remains and the test image shows no new smears; device openings remain dry.

**Common failure modes:** Using shirt fabric or tissue; abrasive material; excessive pressure; spraying cleaner directly; cleaning with grit trapped in cloth.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, inspect before shooting and clean only if needed, avoiding unnecessary contact.

**Transfer task:** Apply the same care principle to a tablet or optical surface using that manufacturer’s instructions.

**Safety and scope:** Power down if required by device guidance. Do not use solvents, compressed air at close range or sharp tools.

**Evidence to record:** Device model, cleaning method, cloth condition and test-image result.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-002 — Choose an aspect ratio intentionally

**Target performance:** Select an image aspect ratio based on subject, composition and target platform while preserving important content.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a practice photo and identify the intended display context before editing.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify where the image will be displayed and the platform’s current recommended dimensions. 2. Inspect the original frame and note important subject boundaries. 3. Compare common ratios such as 1:1, 4:5, 3:2 and 16:9. 4. Preview each crop without overwriting the original. 5. Check whether the crop removes context, limbs, text or horizon information. 6. Choose the ratio that serves the message and display, not merely the trend. 7. Export a copy at the target dimensions and inspect it at phone size.

**Practice dose:** Create three crop variants from one image and compare them at actual display size.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Chosen crop preserves the intended subject and context; no important detail is accidentally clipped; original remains available.

**Common failure modes:** Cropping before deciding purpose; using one ratio for every platform; stretching instead of cropping; ignoring safe areas and interface overlays.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, choose a ratio for a different image and explain the trade-off.

**Transfer task:** Create platform-specific versions from one master image and label them clearly.

**Safety and scope:** Respect image rights and people’s privacy; do not crop to misrepresent an event or remove context deceptively.

**Evidence to record:** Original file, target platform/spec date, crop variants, chosen ratio and export dimensions.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-003 — Use grid lines for alignment

**Target performance:** Use camera grid lines as a compositional aid to align horizons, architecture and subject placement without treating the grid as a rigid rule.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Enable the camera grid if available; choose a stationary scene with a clear horizon or vertical lines.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Enable a grid overlay in camera settings. 2. Identify a dominant horizontal or vertical reference in the scene. 3. Align the horizon or building edge to an appropriate grid line when it supports the composition. 4. Place the subject near a grid intersection only if it improves balance. 5. Take a frame, then deliberately break the alignment for a second frame. 6. Compare which version communicates better. 7. Disable the grid if it distracts or use it only during framing; it does not appear in the final image unless the app specifically exports it.

**Practice dose:** Photograph three scenes: horizon, architecture and a person/object; compare aligned and deliberately offset versions.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Horizon/verticals are intentional and the learner can explain when grid alignment helps or harms.

**Common failure modes:** Tilting the phone; forcing every subject to an intersection; confusing perspective convergence with camera roll; leaving grid enabled as if it were part of the image.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, frame an unfamiliar scene and identify one alignment cue before shooting.

**Transfer task:** Apply grid alignment to a product image and a landscape, explaining their different priorities.

**Safety and scope:** Obtain consent for identifiable people and avoid stepping into unsafe positions to align a shot.

**Evidence to record:** Scene type, grid cue, chosen alignment, comparison image and rationale.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-004 — Avoid digital zoom degradation

**Target performance:** Choose camera position or optical lens selection before digital zoom, recognising how cropping reduces pixel detail.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a phone with its available camera lenses; photograph a static distant subject from a safe location.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify whether the phone offers multiple physical camera modules or only digital zoom. 2. Capture a baseline at the main camera’s native view. 3. If safe and practical, move closer while keeping perspective and subject framing in mind. 4. Compare with a digitally zoomed image from the original position. 5. Inspect detail at the same display size rather than extreme pixel peeping only. 6. Test an optical telephoto lens if the device has one and light is sufficient. 7. Choose the clearest option and avoid digital zoom when it meaningfully degrades detail.

**Practice dose:** Make a three-image comparison: native, physically closer and digital zoom; use a stationary subject.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner correctly identifies which image retains more usable detail and explains the role of crop factor/resolution.

**Common failure modes:** Assuming every zoom number is optical; moving closer without checking safety/perspective; judging only by preview size; ignoring low-light lens limitations.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, choose a capture method for a new subject and explain the trade-off.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a portrait, document and distant landscape; note when moving closer changes perspective or is inappropriate.

**Safety and scope:** Do not approach traffic, wildlife, private property or unsafe edges for a better frame. Respect privacy.

**Evidence to record:** Device lens configuration, zoom setting, distance, image comparison and selection rationale.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-005 — Expose for highlights

**Target performance:** Protect important bright detail by checking exposure and reducing clipping where appropriate while retaining enough shadow information.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a high-contrast static scene such as a bright window and darker interior; use the phone’s standard camera mode.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the brightest important area in the scene. 2. Tap the intended subject and observe the exposure response. 3. Use exposure compensation if available to reduce brightness slightly. 4. Capture a test image and inspect whether highlight texture remains visible. 5. Check that the subject has not become unnecessarily dark. 6. Compare a second exposure and choose the best balance for the intended output. 7. Note that a clipped highlight cannot always be recovered in editing.

**Practice dose:** Take three exposure variants of one high-contrast scene; change only exposure compensation.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** At least one frame retains important highlight detail while keeping the subject readable; learner can identify clipping.

**Common failure modes:** Underexposing everything; exposing for unimportant highlights; assuming editing can restore clipped data; changing white balance and exposure together.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, expose a new high-contrast scene and explain what detail is prioritised.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a bright sky, reflective product and face near a window; priorities differ by subject.

**Safety and scope:** Do not photograph directly into the sun or intense light sources. Respect people’s privacy.

**Evidence to record:** Scene, exposure setting, highlight areas, shadow readability and selected frame.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-006 — Stabilise a handheld phone shot

**Target performance:** Reduce camera shake using posture, grip, support and deliberate movement without blocking lenses or microphones.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a phone and static subject in a safe place; remove bulky accessories that interfere with grip.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Hold the phone with both hands where practical and keep elbows near the torso. 2. Plant feet in a stable stance and avoid leaning over edges. 3. Support the phone against a stable surface when appropriate without obstructing camera or microphone. 4. Press the shutter gently or use a timer to reduce button-induced movement. 5. For video, move slowly from the legs and keep the horizon controlled. 6. Capture a baseline and a stabilised frame/video. 7. Compare sharpness at the same display size.

**Practice dose:** Take five static photos using ordinary grip and five using stabilisation changes; avoid prolonged awkward posture.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Stabilised captures show fewer motion-blur failures than baseline attempts; learner can name the most effective change.

**Common failure modes:** Holding phone at full arm extension; squeezing so hard hands shake; digital stabilisation assumptions; walking backward while filming.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat in a different light level and note that low light requires more stability.

**Transfer task:** Use the method for a short project clip, planning movement and safe stopping points beforehand.

**Safety and scope:** Do not film while driving, crossing roads or walking into hazards. Keep wrists comfortable and use a safe support.

**Evidence to record:** Grip/support method, light level, successful sharp frames and video horizon stability.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-007 — Use leading lines deliberately

**Target performance:** Use visible lines or repeated edges to guide a viewer’s attention toward a subject or through a composition.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a safe public scene with paths, walls, railings or architectural lines; do not trespass for a viewpoint.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify a possible line in the scene and where it leads. 2. Decide whether the line should point toward the subject, create depth or divide the frame. 3. Change viewpoint slightly to strengthen or weaken the line. 4. Check whether the line exits the frame or leads away from the intended subject. 5. Take one frame with the line prominent and one without. 6. Compare viewer attention and depth. 7. Avoid forcing a line into the composition if it creates clutter.

**Practice dose:** Photograph three different line types and compare two viewpoints for each.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** A viewer can identify the intended subject and describe how the line directs attention in at least two examples.

**Common failure modes:** Using any diagonal as a leading line; allowing a bright line to pull attention away; confusing converging perspective with a physical path; ignoring frame edges.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, identify a leading line in an unfamiliar location and predict its effect before shooting.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a product, street scene and portrait background; choose lines that support rather than distract.

**Safety and scope:** Remain in permitted public areas and do not stand in roads, railways or restricted spaces for composition.

**Evidence to record:** Line type, viewpoint, subject position, viewer response and selected frame.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-008 — Separate subject from background

**Target performance:** Improve subject clarity by managing distance, background complexity, contrast, lighting and depth of field.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a stationary object or consenting portrait subject in a safe location; avoid artificial blur for the baseline.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the main subject and inspect the background for competing shapes or bright objects. 2. Move the subject farther from the background when possible. 3. Change camera position to remove tangencies and clutter. 4. Use natural contrast or side light to distinguish edges. 5. Select a suitable focal length/lens and focus on the subject. 6. Compare a wider/deeper-focus frame with a portrait-mode or shallow-depth effect if available. 7. Inspect hair, edges and fine details for artificial blur errors.

**Practice dose:** Create three variants by changing background distance, viewpoint and computational blur separately.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Subject is immediately identifiable and edge separation looks natural; artificial blur does not erase important details.

**Common failure modes:** Relying only on portrait mode; placing bright objects behind the head; creating halos; blurring context that is essential to the story.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, separate a new subject using positioning before relying on editing.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a product image and a portrait, noting that context may be valuable in documentary photography.

**Safety and scope:** Obtain consent for portraits and do not use editing to misrepresent a person or remove meaningful context.

**Evidence to record:** Subject/background distance, viewpoint, light, blur method, edge artefacts and viewer response.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-009 — Edit exposure without destroying detail

**Target performance:** Make restrained exposure and tonal adjustments on a copy while preserving highlight/shadow information and a natural appearance.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a duplicate of a non-sensitive image and a reputable editor; preserve the original untouched.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Duplicate the original and confirm the copy is separate. 2. Inspect overall exposure before changing colour or sharpness. 3. Adjust exposure in small increments. 4. Use highlights and shadows controls only to recover detail that exists in the file. 5. Check histogram or clipping warnings if available. 6. Compare edited and original at normal viewing size. 7. Avoid extreme contrast, shadow lifting noise and excessive clarity. 8. Export a copy and reopen it to confirm the result.

**Practice dose:** Edit two images, changing one tonal control at a time; keep an original for comparison.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Edited image improves readability while retaining natural tonal transitions and no new obvious clipping or noise; original remains preserved.

**Common failure modes:** Over-editing; lifting black shadows excessively; assuming clipped data can be restored; editing a compressed copy repeatedly.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, revisit the edit with fresh eyes and compare against the original before deciding whether to keep it.

**Transfer task:** Create two versions for different display conditions while retaining the same source and documenting adjustments.

**Safety and scope:** Do not edit evidence images in ways that misrepresent factual events. Respect privacy and image rights.

**Evidence to record:** Original/copy paths, editor, adjustment values, clipping/noise check and export verification.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-11-010 — Export an image for a target platform

**Target performance:** Export an image with dimensions, format, colour and file size appropriate to its intended use while retaining a high-quality master.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a practice image and consult the target platform’s current specifications; platform limits change.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify destination and current required/recommended dimensions and file-size limits. 2. Keep a high-quality master separate from delivery copies. 3. Choose JPEG for common photographic delivery or PNG when transparency/graphic edges require it; choose other formats only when supported. 4. Resize to the target dimensions without stretching. 5. Select a reasonable quality setting and colour profile supported by the platform. 6. Use a clear filename and export folder. 7. Reopen the exported file and inspect dimensions, orientation, colour and compression artefacts. 8. Upload only after checking privacy and rights.

**Practice dose:** Export one photo for two different destinations and compare file size and quality.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Both files meet the destination’s current requirements, display correctly and preserve the master.

**Common failure modes:** Using outdated specs; overwriting master; exporting at wrong orientation; excessive compression; including private metadata unintentionally.

**Delayed retention check:** After a month or platform update, recheck specs and repeat with a new image.

**Transfer task:** Build a reusable export preset and record the platform/spec date so it can be reviewed later.

**Safety and scope:** Remove location metadata when it could expose sensitive information. Do not upload images without permission or rights.

**Evidence to record:** Platform/spec source and date, dimensions, format, file size, metadata settings and reopened-file check.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-001 — Find a comfortable speaking pitch

**Target performance:** Identify a relaxed conversational pitch range without forcing the voice higher or lower than is comfortable.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a quiet room and short ordinary speech; optional tuner/pitch app is secondary, not a medical measure.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Speak a few natural sentences at normal volume. 2. Notice where the voice feels easiest and most stable. 3. Hum gently on a comfortable note without pushing for a target frequency. 4. Repeat after a short rest and compare comfort. 5. Try a small upward and downward glide within an easy range. 6. Stop before strain, breathiness or throat tension appears. 7. Record a short sample and note comfort rather than judging voice identity.

**Practice dose:** Practise 2–3 minutes, two or three times weekly; no extended pitch drills.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner can identify a comfortable conversational region and produce short speech without strain; no fixed pitch number is required.

**Common failure modes:** Forcing a deeper or higher voice; copying another speaker; treating pitch apps as diagnosis; continuing through hoarseness.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat ordinary speech and note whether comfort remains consistent.

**Transfer task:** Use the comfortable range for a short presentation and prioritise clarity and naturalness over pitch manipulation.

**Safety and scope:** Stop for pain, persistent hoarseness or vocal fatigue. Seek qualified clinical advice for persistent voice concerns; this is not voice therapy.

**Evidence to record:** Short recording, subjective comfort, optional pitch range estimate and any strain signs.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-002 — Articulate consonant endings clearly

**Target performance:** Produce word-final consonants audibly in connected speech without adding unnatural extra vowels or over-enunciating.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a short passage containing varied final consonants; use ordinary speaking volume.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Read the passage silently and mark words with final consonants. 2. Speak slowly, completing the final sound while keeping the word natural. 3. Record a short sample. 4. Listen for omitted endings or added vowel sounds. 5. Practise minimal pairs or short phrases that contrast final sounds. 6. Return to normal sentence pace and preserve meaning. 7. Ask a listener to transcribe a few target words without seeing the text.

**Practice dose:** Practise 3–5 minutes with short phrases; avoid repetitive strain or exaggerated mouth movement.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Listener correctly identifies at least 8 of 10 target words and speech remains natural at conversational pace.

**Common failure modes:** Adding an extra vowel after every consonant; speaking unnaturally slowly; shouting; focusing on sound at the expense of meaning.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, read a fresh passage and compare listener transcription accuracy.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a short English or Bangla explanation, noting that sound patterns and acceptable articulation differ by language.

**Safety and scope:** Use comfortable volume and stop if jaw/throat discomfort occurs. Do not treat accent difference as a defect.

**Evidence to record:** Passage, target words, listener transcription, recording and language-specific notes.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-003 — Maintain a steady beat

**Target performance:** Tap or vocalise a consistent pulse and recover after a brief interruption without accelerating.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a metronome or simple music excerpt at a comfortable moderate tempo; keep volume low.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Listen to the pulse before tapping. 2. Tap a steady beat with one hand or foot. 3. Count evenly for eight beats. 4. Continue through a short pause or missing sound while maintaining internal timing. 5. Compare with a metronome or recording. 6. If timing drifts, slow down and restart rather than forcing speed. 7. Repeat at a second moderate tempo.

**Practice dose:** Two short rounds of 30–60 seconds; stop if tapping causes discomfort.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Maintain the pulse for 30 seconds with only minor drift and rejoin accurately after a brief pause.

**Common failure modes:** Following every note rather than pulse; speeding up; tapping too hard; practising at an excessively fast tempo.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, maintain a beat with a different sound or music excerpt.

**Transfer task:** Apply pulse to a simple spoken rhythm or beginner music exercise, distinguishing beat from subdivision.

**Safety and scope:** Keep audio at safe volume and do not practise while crossing roads or operating equipment.

**Evidence to record:** Tempo/source, duration, drift observation, interruption recovery and delayed result.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-004 — Hear simple pitch direction

**Target performance:** Distinguish whether a second tone is higher, lower or approximately the same as a reference tone.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a safe tone generator, instrument or keyboard at moderate volume and comfortable pitch range.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Play a reference tone and listen without singing. 2. Play a second tone a small distance away. 3. Identify higher, lower or same before checking. 4. Repeat with intervals presented in random order. 5. Include a few same-pitch trials to prevent guessing by contrast. 6. Record uncertain responses rather than forcing an answer. 7. Gradually widen or narrow the interval depending on accuracy.

**Practice dose:** Use 10–15 trials per session, two or three times weekly; keep the range comfortable.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identify direction in at least 8 of 10 trials across more than one session; same-pitch trials are included.

**Common failure modes:** Using loudness as a cue; memorising sequence order; using extreme pitches; treating one session as proof of perfect pitch.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, complete a new randomised set with different reference tones.

**Transfer task:** Use pitch direction to learn a short two- or three-note motif, then verify by listening rather than visual display alone.

**Safety and scope:** Keep playback moderate. Stop if tones cause discomfort or tinnitus-like symptoms.

**Evidence to record:** Tone source/range, trial sequence, responses, accuracy and delayed test.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-005 — Sing a short phrase in tune

**Target performance:** Reproduce a short, comfortable melodic phrase with broadly accurate pitch and rhythm without forcing volume or range.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a simple phrase within a comfortable range; use a reference recording or instrument.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Listen to the phrase once for overall contour. 2. Hum the melody gently before singing words. 3. Break it into two small units and match each to the reference. 4. Join the units while keeping the rhythm steady. 5. Record one attempt and compare pitch direction and phrase endings. 6. Correct one difficult note at a time. 7. Sing the phrase at comfortable volume and stop before fatigue.

**Practice dose:** Practise 3–5 minutes, with rests; do not repeat the same phrase until the voice tires.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Phrase contour and rhythm are recognisable and most notes are close to the reference; learner remains comfortable.

**Common failure modes:** Singing too loudly; forcing high notes; ignoring rhythm; repeating through hoarseness; relying on pitch display without listening.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, sing the phrase without hearing the reference immediately beforehand, then compare.

**Transfer task:** Learn a second short phrase with a different contour and explain which interval was harder.

**Safety and scope:** No belting or forced range extension. Stop for pain, strain or persistent hoarseness; consult qualified help for ongoing issues.

**Evidence to record:** Phrase/source, comfortable range, recording, pitch/rhythm observations and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-006 — Use a clean vocal onset

**Target performance:** Begin a spoken or sung phrase without a harsh glottal attack or excessive breathiness, using a comfortable natural start.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use gentle humming and short vowel sounds at normal volume; avoid sustained high notes.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Relax jaw, shoulders and neck. 2. Take a natural, quiet breath without a large gasp. 3. Begin with a gentle hum, then transition to a vowel. 4. Notice whether the onset is abrupt, airy or balanced. 5. Repeat with a short spoken phrase, keeping the start easy. 6. Record one sample and listen for a hard attack or excessive breath leak. 7. Stop if the voice feels irritated; do not force a particular technique.

**Practice dose:** Two or three minutes of gentle practice, a few times weekly.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Phrase begins comfortably without obvious harsh attack or excessive airiness in a short sample; no discomfort.

**Common failure modes:** Pushing from the throat; taking a huge breath; whispering to avoid onset; treating the exercise as therapy for a voice disorder.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, use a fresh phrase and assess comfort and clarity.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a presentation opening and a simple sung phrase, keeping the onset natural for the task.

**Safety and scope:** Voice technique is not medical treatment. Stop for pain or hoarseness; persistent symptoms need qualified clinical assessment.

**Evidence to record:** Phrase sample, comfort, perceived onset quality and delayed comparison.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-007 — Pace a spoken explanation

**Target performance:** Adjust speaking rate and pauses so a listener can follow a short explanation without losing key relationships.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a familiar 60–90 second topic and a willing listener.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the main message and three supporting points. 2. Mark natural pause locations between ideas. 3. Deliver once at normal pace and ask the listener to state the main point. 4. Identify any rushed section or unnecessary pause. 5. Repeat with a deliberate pause before the central conclusion and slower pace for complex terms. 6. Keep simple material moving; do not slow every sentence equally. 7. Ask the listener which version was easier to follow.

**Practice dose:** Two takes per session; use one specific pacing adjustment each time.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Listener accurately recalls the main point and at least two supporting ideas; speech remains natural and within the intended time.

**Common failure modes:** Speaking at one speed throughout; pausing mid-phrase; slowing so much that structure is lost; using filler words to fill silence.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, explain a new topic and adapt pace to listener questions.

**Transfer task:** Use pacing in a classroom explanation and a short project presentation; note audience differences.

**Safety and scope:** Use comfortable volume; do not force breath or continue if voice is tired.

**Evidence to record:** Recording, duration, listener recall, pacing change and feedback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-008 — Listen for phrase structure in music

**Target performance:** Identify where a musical phrase begins, develops and resolves by listening for repetition, cadence, breath or melodic grouping.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a short, moderate-volume musical excerpt with a clear melody.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Listen once for the overall mood and pulse. 2. Listen again and mark where the melody seems to form a complete musical thought. 3. Notice repeated motifs, pauses, harmonic changes or points of resolution. 4. Hum the contour softly if useful. 5. Compare phrase lengths and identify whether they repeat or contrast. 6. Check your interpretation with a score or knowledgeable listener if available. 7. State uncertainty where phrase boundaries are ambiguous.

**Practice dose:** Analyse two excerpts of 30–60 seconds; do not over-repeat one passage.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Identify at least two plausible phrase boundaries and explain the audible cues supporting them.

**Common failure modes:** Equating every bar with a phrase; relying only on silence; confusing beat grouping with melodic phrase; asserting one boundary as universal.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, mark phrase structure in an unfamiliar excerpt and compare with a second listener.

**Transfer task:** Use phrase awareness to shape breathing and expression in a short vocal performance without altering the melody.

**Safety and scope:** Keep playback moderate and respect copyright when sharing excerpts.

**Evidence to record:** Excerpt/source, timestamps, phrase marks, cues and comparison notes.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-009 — Project speech without shouting

**Target performance:** Make speech audible to a nearby audience through clear articulation, posture and direction rather than excessive throat force.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a quiet room and a listener several metres away; no amplified auditorium required.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Stand or sit with a balanced, comfortable posture. 2. Face the listener and articulate the message clearly. 3. Use natural breath and moderate volume. 4. Direct the voice toward the listener without pushing the throat. 5. Ask the listener to repeat key words and rate audibility. 6. Adjust distance, room position or microphone before increasing vocal force. 7. Stop if the voice feels strained.

**Practice dose:** Practise a 30–60 second message in two room positions; rest between attempts.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Listener hears and understands the message at ordinary room distance without the speaker shouting or feeling strain.

**Common failure modes:** Equating projection with loudness; lifting chin and tightening neck; speaking over noise rather than changing environment; ignoring microphone options.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, speak to a small group in a different room and assess audibility and comfort.

**Transfer task:** Use in a presentation by checking room acoustics and microphone before changing vocal effort.

**Safety and scope:** No shouting drills. Stop for pain, hoarseness or fatigue; persistent symptoms require professional assessment.

**Evidence to record:** Room/distance, listener audibility, comprehension, volume comfort and delayed result.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-12-010 — Record clean phone audio

**Target performance:** Capture intelligible speech on a phone by controlling microphone distance, background noise, handling and file verification.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use the phone’s built-in recorder in a quiet room; record a non-sensitive test passage.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify microphone locations for the device and avoid covering them. 2. Choose a quiet room and reduce nearby fans or handling noise where possible. 3. Place the phone at a consistent comfortable distance; do not press it against the mouth. 4. Record a 10-second test and listen through headphones at moderate volume. 5. Check clipping, echo, hum and plosive wind noise. 6. Adjust distance or orientation one variable at a time. 7. Record the full passage and save with a descriptive filename. 8. Reopen the file and verify playback before sharing.

**Practice dose:** Make three short test recordings changing only distance or orientation; then record one final sample.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Speech is intelligible, not clipped, and free of dominant handling noise; saved file reopens and plays.

**Common failure modes:** Covering microphone; recording too close; holding phone while rubbing case; using noisy room; failing to verify file.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, record in a second room and compare noise and intelligibility.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a voice note or project narration, choosing compression/format only after confirming platform requirements.

**Safety and scope:** Do not record people without consent. Avoid sharing private conversations or sensitive audio; keep volume safe.

**Evidence to record:** Device/app, distance, room, settings, sample file check and intelligibility feedback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.
