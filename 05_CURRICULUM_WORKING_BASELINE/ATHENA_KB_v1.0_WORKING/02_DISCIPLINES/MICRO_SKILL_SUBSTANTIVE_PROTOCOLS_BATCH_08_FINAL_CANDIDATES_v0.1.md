# ATHENA Substantive Micro-skill Protocols — Batch 08

**Status:** Substantive authored drafts; not independently reviewed, calibrated or certified.

**Scope:** Final 35 candidate protocols across dexterity, household independence, personal operations and legacy micro-skills. Safety-critical skills remain restricted to supervised or qualified instruction.


## MS-02-001 — Ambidexterity Basics (longitudinal non-dominant-hand use)

**Target performance:** Build useful, comfortable competence in selected everyday tasks with the non-dominant hand over time; equal-handedness is not the goal.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose one low-risk daily task and establish a baseline. Do not begin with knives, hot objects, tools, sports contact or other safety-critical tasks.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Record the dominant hand and current non-dominant-hand performance for one chosen task. 2. Select a simple anchor such as brushing teeth gently, using a phone stylus or moving light objects. 3. Practise slowly for 1–3 minutes, prioritising control and comfort. 4. Stop if grip, wrist or shoulder becomes strained. 5. Keep the same task for several days before adding another. 6. Once reliable, introduce a second low-risk task such as drawing simple shapes. 7. Review performance weekly and retain successful tasks with occasional ordinary use. 8. Do not force a switch in tasks where precision or safety depends on the dominant hand.

**Practice dose:** Short practice 3–5 days per week; one or two tasks at a time. Treat progress as a multi-month habit, not a quick challenge.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** For a selected task, show improved control and consistency compared with baseline across multiple weeks, without pain or disruption to essential daily tasks.

**Common failure modes:** Trying to train every task at once; using the weaker hand for knives/hot liquids; judging progress only by speed; practising through pain.

**Delayed retention check:** Compare the same task after 1, 4 and 8 weeks; keep history even if performance fluctuates.

**Transfer task:** Transfer to a new low-risk fine-motor task only after the anchor task is comfortable; retain the original skill through normal use.

**Safety and scope:** No requirement to become equally skilled with both hands. Stop for pain or numbness. Do not change hands for safety-critical tasks or where it creates risk.

**Evidence to record:** Baseline and dated samples, task/duration, comfort, consistency and longitudinal review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-002 — Coin Rolling (finger roll and controlled transfer)

**Target performance:** Move a coin across the fingers with controlled, repeatable motion and recover it safely without throwing or dropping it into hazards.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a large, smooth coin or practice token while seated over a clear surface. Keep small objects away from younger children and pets.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Rest the coin between thumb and index finger. 2. Use the middle finger to receive the coin as the index finger releases pressure. 3. Transfer the coin across the finger joints in small controlled stages. 4. Keep the wrist relaxed and use minimal movement. 5. Practise one transfer at a time before attempting a continuous roll. 6. If the coin drops, stop and retrieve it rather than chasing it. 7. Practise both directions only after one direction is controlled.

**Practice dose:** Three sets of 1–2 minutes with rests; stop if hand cramps.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete five controlled transfers in sequence without forceful flicking and retrieve the coin safely if dropped.

**Common failure modes:** Trying to move too quickly; using a coin too small; excessive wrist motion; practising over drains or near electronics.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, perform the same sequence without looking continuously at the fingers.

**Transfer task:** Transfer the movement to a larger practice token or a different hand position without increasing speed first.

**Safety and scope:** Avoid use near mouths, drains, moving machinery or small children. Stop for pain/cramping.

**Evidence to record:** Token size, successful transfers, drops, hand used and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-003 — Pen Spinning (safe basic rotations)

**Target performance:** Perform a slow, controlled basic pen rotation around the thumb or between fingers using a lightweight capped practice pen.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a lightweight, non-sharp, capped pen over a desk. No knives, metal darts or weighted improvised objects.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Choose a balanced, capped pen that is not slippery. 2. Learn the starting grip and identify the receiving finger position. 3. Practise the push with minimal force so the pen rotates rather than flies away. 4. Catch the pen with relaxed fingers and reset. 5. Repeat as isolated single rotations before linking attempts. 6. Keep the movement over a clear desk and away from faces. 7. Stop when grip becomes tense or control declines.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of 10–15 attempts with rest; prioritise controlled catches over speed.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete five single rotations with safe catches and no forceful launches.

**Common failure modes:** Using sharp pens; excessive force; practising near eyes or screens; attempting advanced combos before a basic catch is stable.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, perform a few controlled rotations while maintaining normal posture.

**Transfer task:** Learn a second simple rotation only after the first is reliable; explain how pen balance affects movement.

**Safety and scope:** Use only capped, non-sharp pens; do not spin near faces, other people, fragile objects or while walking.

**Evidence to record:** Pen type, hand, successful rotations/catches and control notes.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-004 — Everyday Knot Tying (foundational knots)

**Target performance:** Select and tie a small set of ordinary utility knots for appropriate non-life-safety tasks and verify each knot’s suitability.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use soft practice rope of manageable thickness. Choose knots for bundles, temporary tying or craft—not climbing, rescue, towing or securing people.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the task and load; reject any task involving human support, lifting or safety-critical restraint. 2. Learn the overhand knot as a simple stopper and the square knot only for joining similar-size ends under suitable conditions. 3. Practise tying slowly while naming each strand movement. 4. Dress the knot by removing twists and arranging strands. 5. Apply only a gentle hand check appropriate to the practice task. 6. Learn how the knot can jam or slip. 7. Untie and inspect the rope after each attempt.

**Practice dose:** Practise two knots for 5–10 minutes; learn one purpose and limitation for each.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Tie each selected knot correctly three times and explain a task where it should not be used.

**Common failure modes:** Confusing square and granny knots; using a knot beyond its rating; ignoring rope material; treating a hand check as certification.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, tie from memory and explain the limitation before using it.

**Transfer task:** Compare two knots for a simple bundle and justify the safer choice for that non-critical purpose.

**Safety and scope:** Never use these practice knots for climbing, rescue, lifting, towing, fall protection or securing a person/animal. Consult qualified instruction for safety-critical ropework.

**Evidence to record:** Knot name, task, rope type, dressing, limitation and repeated attempts.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-005 — Knot tying: bowline

**Target performance:** Tie and identify a basic bowline for demonstration and non-load-bearing practice while understanding that knot use is application- and rope-dependent.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use soft practice rope on a desk. This protocol is not for climbing, rescue, boating, lifting or any life-safety application.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the standing part and working end. 2. Form a small loop in the standing part with the intended orientation. 3. Pass the working end through the loop, around the standing part and back through the loop. 4. Dress the knot so the strands lie neatly without crossing incorrectly. 5. Compare the finished structure with a trusted knot diagram or instructor demonstration. 6. Practise untying and retie slowly. 7. Learn that different bowline variants and backup methods exist and that visual similarity does not establish suitability.

**Practice dose:** Five slow ties on practice rope; check each against a trusted diagram.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Three consecutive knots match the reference structure and learner can identify standing part, working end and the knot’s limits.

**Common failure modes:** Reversing the loop; failing to dress the knot; assuming a bowline cannot loosen; using it for human support.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, tie from memory and have another person compare it with the reference.

**Transfer task:** Explain why safety-critical rope use requires a qualified course, suitable rope and application-specific inspection.

**Safety and scope:** Never use this practice protocol for climbing, rescue, lifting, towing, fall protection, water safety or securing a person.

**Evidence to record:** Reference source, rope type, knot checks, errors and independent visual verification.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-006 — Origami (foundational folds and model sequence)

**Target performance:** Produce accurate foundational folds and complete a simple model by following a sequence while maintaining crease alignment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use square paper on a clean flat surface. Start with a simple model from a reputable diagram; avoid sharp tools.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Confirm the paper is square and identify colour/front side. 2. Align corners or edges precisely before making a crease. 3. Press the crease from centre outward to reduce drift. 4. Distinguish valley and mountain folds using the diagram convention. 5. Complete one step at a time and check orientation before continuing. 6. If a fold is wrong, unfold gently rather than forcing the paper. 7. Compare the final model with the reference from several angles.

**Practice dose:** One simple model per session; repeat the same model once before progressing.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete the model with major edges aligned and no major unintended tears; explain the fold sequence without relying solely on memory.

**Common failure modes:** Folding before aligning corners; reversing model orientation; pressing too hard before checking; skipping steps.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, recreate the model from the diagram and compare accuracy.

**Transfer task:** Use the same folding precision for a more complex model, adding one new fold type at a time.

**Safety and scope:** Use paper only; keep fingers clear of any optional cutting tools and do not use blades as part of this protocol.

**Evidence to record:** Model name/source, paper size, fold errors, final photos and delayed recreation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-007 — Handwriting legibility under time

**Target performance:** Write a short passage at a practical pace while preserving readable letter forms, spacing and line alignment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use comfortable pen/paper and a short passage at an appropriate level. Establish a baseline before timing.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Write a 2-minute untimed sample at a comfortable pace. 2. Identify the main legibility issue: letter formation, spacing, size, slant or line drift. 3. Practise one targeted pattern for a few minutes. 4. Write the same length passage at a modest time target, not maximum speed. 5. Compare readability of letters and word spacing. 6. Reduce pace if clarity collapses. 7. Adjust grip and posture for comfort.

**Practice dose:** Two short samples per session, 5–8 minutes total; avoid long repetitive drills.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** A reader unfamiliar with the text can transcribe it accurately; speed increases only if legibility and comfort remain stable.

**Common failure modes:** Pressing too hard; rushing; inconsistent letter size; copying a style that strains the hand; using speed as the only measure.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, write a fresh passage at the same target and compare legibility with baseline.

**Transfer task:** Apply to timed note-taking by using concise wording and headings rather than forcing handwriting speed alone.

**Safety and scope:** Stop for pain, numbness or cramping; use comfortable posture and appropriate pen grip.

**Evidence to record:** Timed sample, words/minute if useful, reader transcription accuracy, comfort and error pattern.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-008 — Keyboard touch-typing home row

**Target performance:** Use home-row finger positions and return fingers to a stable baseline while typing short text with increasing accuracy.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a keyboard with a comfortable layout and a typing practice tool. Begin with a short session.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the home-row keys for the keyboard layout and tactile markers if present. 2. Place fingers lightly on the home row and relax shoulders. 3. Practise short sequences using only the assigned fingers. 4. Return fingers to home position after each reach. 5. Add top/bottom row reaches gradually while maintaining finger mapping. 6. Type short words and sentences with accuracy as the primary metric. 7. Review errors and practise only the weakest key transitions.

**Practice dose:** 5–10 minutes per session, 3–5 days per week; stop before wrist or finger fatigue.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Accuracy reaches at least 95% on a short familiar practice set before speed is increased; hands remain relaxed.

**Common failure modes:** Looking down constantly; pressing keys hard; using incorrect fingers; chasing words-per-minute at the cost of accuracy.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, type a new passage without looking at the keyboard and compare error rate.

**Transfer task:** Transfer to coding or Bangla/English typing by learning the layout-specific keys and shortcuts separately.

**Safety and scope:** Use neutral wrist posture and breaks; stop for pain or numbness. Keyboard layouts differ; do not assume a single mapping for all languages.

**Evidence to record:** Keyboard/layout, practice text, accuracy, speed, error types and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-009 — Mouse / trackpad precision

**Target performance:** Select, drag and place interface targets accurately with controlled pointer movement and minimal unintended clicks.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a harmless practice interface or blank drawing canvas; adjust pointer speed to a comfortable level.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Set pointer speed so small hand movements remain controllable. 2. Practise moving to large targets and clicking once. 3. Progress to smaller targets without rushing. 4. Practise double-click timing only where needed. 5. Drag an object slowly and release within a target area. 6. Use trackpad gestures only after learning the device’s supported gestures. 7. Review missed clicks and adjust speed or hand position.

**Practice dose:** 5 minutes of target practice; include short breaks and alternate hand position if uncomfortable.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Select 9 of 10 medium targets and complete several drag tasks without accidental activation of nearby controls.

**Common failure modes:** Pointer speed too high; gripping mouse tightly; resting finger on button; dragging when a simple click is required.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, complete a new target set and compare error rate.

**Transfer task:** Apply to file management or diagram editing, practising on copies rather than live destructive controls.

**Safety and scope:** Use ergonomic posture and stop if wrist/hand pain develops. Do not practise on payment, delete or account-security screens.

**Evidence to record:** Device, pointer settings, target accuracy, drag success and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-02-010 — Basic ball wall pass control

**Target performance:** Pass a ball against a safe wall and control the return using appropriate touch and spacing.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a suitable ball, clear open area and permission to use the wall. Ensure windows, people and traffic are out of range.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Stand a short distance from the wall with balanced posture. 2. Pass gently using the inside of the foot toward a broad target. 3. Track the rebound and move into its path. 4. Cushion the return with the inside of the foot, keeping it within one or two steps. 5. Repeat slowly and alternate feet when control is stable. 6. Increase distance or pace one variable at a time. 7. Stop if the ball rebounds unpredictably or the area becomes unsafe.

**Practice dose:** Two sets of 8–10 gentle passes with rest; no high-power striking.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete at least 7 of 10 pass-and-control cycles with a manageable return and no unsafe rebound.

**Common failure modes:** Standing too close; striking too hard; stiff first touch; using a wall not intended for ball practice; ignoring rebound direction.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, repeat at the same distance and compare control with both feet.

**Transfer task:** Use the control pattern with a partner, adding movement only after wall control is consistent.

**Safety and scope:** Use an appropriate ball and permitted space; avoid roads, windows and public walkways. Stop for pain or unsafe conditions.

**Evidence to record:** Ball type, distance, foot, successful cycles, rebound errors and comfort.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-001 — Wash hands using a complete routine

**Target performance:** Perform handwashing with soap and clean running water for an adequate duration, covering all hand surfaces and drying safely.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a functioning sink, soap and clean towel or drying method.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Wet hands with clean running water. 2. Apply soap. 3. Rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers, thumbs, fingertips/nails and wrists as appropriate. 4. Continue scrubbing for at least 20 seconds. 5. Rinse thoroughly under clean running water. 6. Dry with a clean towel or air dryer. 7. Avoid recontaminating hands by touching dirty taps or surfaces; use a clean barrier where appropriate. 8. Repeat at key times such as after toilet use, before food preparation and after coughing/sneezing.

**Practice dose:** Use at relevant daily moments rather than as a timed performance drill; follow public-health guidance.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** All hand surfaces are covered for the recommended duration and hands are rinsed and dried; learner can identify key moments for washing.

**Common failure modes:** Rinsing without soap; missing thumbs/fingertips; washing too briefly; touching contaminated surfaces immediately after washing.

**Delayed retention check:** Observe routine consistency over a week and correct missed areas without excessive washing that irritates skin.

**Transfer task:** Explain when soap and water are preferable to hand sanitiser according to local public-health advice.

**Safety and scope:** Use skin-safe soap; dry hands; seek adult advice for severe irritation or skin conditions. Do not share towels when hygiene is uncertain.

**Evidence to record:** Routine checklist, duration estimate, missed areas and weekly consistency.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-002 — Read a food date and storage label

**Target performance:** Interpret date marking, storage instructions and after-opening guidance without confusing quality dates with safety guarantees.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use sealed food packaging and current local food-safety guidance. Do not taste questionable food as a test.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Locate the date marking and identify its wording (for example, use-by, best-before or local equivalent). 2. Read storage temperature and refrigeration/freezing instructions. 3. Check after-opening duration and preparation requirements. 4. Inspect packaging integrity and signs of damage or swelling without opening. 5. Consider whether the product has been stored as instructed. 6. If date meaning or safety is uncertain, ask a responsible adult and follow local food-safety guidance. 7. Do not rely on smell or appearance alone to establish safety.

**Practice dose:** Read five different labels with an adult; compare chilled, shelf-stable and frozen products.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identify date type, storage condition and after-opening instruction for each sample; flags uncertainty rather than guessing.

**Common failure modes:** Treating best-before and use-by as identical; ignoring temperature; assuming smell proves safety; consuming damaged packaging.

**Delayed retention check:** After a few weeks, read a new product label and explain the storage chain required.

**Transfer task:** Apply to meal planning by selecting foods that can be stored safely within the available facilities.

**Safety and scope:** Never taste food to test safety. When in doubt, do not consume; ask a responsible adult and follow official food-safety guidance.

**Evidence to record:** Product type, date wording, storage instructions, packaging condition and adult review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-003 — Prepare a simple no-heat snack hygienically

**Target performance:** Assemble a simple snack without heating while controlling hand hygiene, clean surfaces, allergen awareness and food storage.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a familiar no-heat recipe approved by a responsible adult. Check allergies and ingredient labels first.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Wash hands and clear a clean preparation surface. 2. Gather ingredients and utensils before opening packages. 3. Read allergen and storage information; do not use an ingredient if safety is uncertain. 4. Rinse produce under clean running water where appropriate. 5. Use clean utensils and keep ready-to-eat food separate from raw animal products. 6. Portion ingredients and assemble the snack. 7. Refrigerate perishable leftovers promptly according to local guidance. 8. Clean the area and store remaining ingredients correctly.

**Practice dose:** Prepare one simple snack under appropriate supervision; focus on safe sequence, not speed.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Snack is assembled with clean utensils, ingredients are suitable and safely stored, and workspace is cleaned afterward.

**Common failure modes:** Skipping handwashing; cross-contamination; ignoring allergens; leaving perishable food at room temperature; using unwashed produce.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat a different simple snack after a week and identify what hygiene steps stay constant.

**Transfer task:** Adapt the procedure to a balanced snack using available foods while respecting dietary needs and guardian guidance.

**Safety and scope:** Check allergies and choking hazards; adult supervision as appropriate. No knives, heat or unknown ingredients in this protocol.

**Evidence to record:** Recipe, ingredients, label/allergen check, hygiene sequence, storage and cleanup.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-004 — Use a kitchen knife safely under supervision

**Target performance:** Use a suitable kitchen knife for a simple supervised food-preparation task with stable board, controlled grip and safe storage.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Direct adult supervision is mandatory. Use a suitable sharp, maintained kitchen knife and stable cutting board; do not practise alone.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Clear the workspace and place a damp non-slip mat under the board if appropriate. 2. Inspect knife handle and blade; use the correct knife for the task. 3. Stand with stable posture and keep the workpiece secure. 4. Learn the claw grip from the supervising adult, keeping fingertips curled away from the blade. 5. Make slow, controlled cuts away from the body and never catch a falling knife. 6. Stop and reposition food when it becomes too small or unstable. 7. Place the knife flat and visible when not in use; wash and store it under adult direction.

**Practice dose:** One short supervised task using soft, stable food; no speed or advanced cutting challenge.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Completes the agreed cuts with stable board, controlled grip and no near miss; supervisor confirms safe handling.

**Common failure modes:** Holding food with fingertips extended; cutting toward hand/body; catching a falling knife; leaving knife in sink; using dull or unsuitable blade.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat only under supervision; safety must be observed each time, not assumed from one successful session.

**Transfer task:** Explain which tasks require an adult or a different tool; do not progress to advanced techniques without instruction.

**Safety and scope:** High-risk tool. Adult directly supervises throughout. No solo practice, knife tricks, blade throwing or use when distracted/tired.

**Evidence to record:** Supervisor, task, knife/board setup, observed technique, near misses and safe storage.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-005 — Measure dry and liquid ingredients

**Target performance:** Measure ingredients using appropriate tools and consistent units while distinguishing volume from mass.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a simple adult-approved recipe, measuring cups/spoons and a kitchen scale if available.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Read the recipe and identify each unit. 2. Choose a suitable tool: measuring jug for liquid volume, spoons/cups for specified volume, scale for mass. 3. Place the tool on a level surface. 4. Fill dry ingredients using the recipe’s specified method and level only when instructed. 5. Read liquid at eye level with the jug on a flat surface. 6. Tare a scale before weighing and record units. 7. Keep ingredients separate until each measurement is checked.

**Practice dose:** Measure five ingredients for one simple recipe; repeat one measurement to compare consistency.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Measurements match the recipe within the tool’s resolution; units are recorded and liquid readings are taken at eye level.

**Common failure modes:** Confusing grams and millilitres; packing flour when not instructed; reading jug from above; forgetting tare.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, measure a different recipe and explain why ingredient density matters.

**Transfer task:** Scale a recipe proportionally using arithmetic, checking that units and practical limits remain sensible.

**Safety and scope:** Follow adult supervision and food hygiene. Do not use unverified substitutions for allergens or medically restricted diets.

**Evidence to record:** Recipe, tool, target and measured values, units, repeat difference and errors.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-006 — Clean a surface using the correct product

**Target performance:** Select an appropriate cleaning method from the surface and product label, apply it safely and avoid dangerous chemical mixing.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a routine low-risk surface and a household-approved product under adult guidance. Keep the container closed until ready.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the surface material and visible soil. 2. Read the product label for intended surface, dilution, contact time and precautions. 3. Ventilate as directed and use required protection. 4. Apply product according to label instructions; never mix products. 5. Use a clean cloth or tool and work from cleaner areas toward dirtier areas where appropriate. 6. Respect required contact time and rinse only if label instructs. 7. Store product securely and wash hands. 8. If product suitability is uncertain, use a safer adult-approved method.

**Practice dose:** Clean one small surface with adult approval; no experimentation with different chemicals.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Surface is cleaned without damage, residue or unsafe exposure; learner can state the label’s key precaution and storage rule.

**Common failure modes:** Mixing bleach with acids/ammonia; using wrong product on a surface; ignoring ventilation/contact time; transferring chemicals into unlabelled bottles.

**Delayed retention check:** Review label before a later use and check whether product instructions or surface material changed.

**Transfer task:** Apply the selection method to a second surface and explain why one product may be unsuitable.

**Safety and scope:** Never mix cleaners. Keep chemicals away from food, children and pets; do not spray near face. Follow label and adult directions; exposure requires immediate adult/appropriate emergency guidance.

**Evidence to record:** Product label, surface, precautions, method, outcome and storage check.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-13-007 — Sort laundry by care requirements

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, sort a new set and check any uncertain label using a trusted care-symbol guide.

**Transfer task:** Create a simple household laundry guide that records common garment-specific instructions with permission.

**Safety and scope:** Use detergents only as labelled and under adult guidance. Keep pods/chemicals away from children and never ingest them.

**Evidence to record:** Garment type, care symbol, group, uncertainty and adult confirmation.

## MS-13-008 — Fold and store common clothing

**Target performance:** Fold and store everyday garments in a way that preserves shape, fits available space and makes items easy to retrieve.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use clean, dry garments and a clean storage area; respect household preferences.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify garment type and whether hanging or folding is preferable. 2. Smooth the garment gently on a clean surface. 3. Fold along seams or natural garment lines to reduce bulky creases. 4. Keep paired items together where useful. 5. Store heavier items so they do not crush delicate garments. 6. Arrange by category or use frequency. 7. Leave enough space to retrieve items without collapsing the stack. 8. Review after use and return garments to the same category.

**Practice dose:** Fold a small group of three garment types; use a consistent method per type.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, retrieve and return several items and see whether the organisation remains usable.

**Transfer task:** Adapt storage to a drawer, shelf or travel bag without damaging garments.

**Safety and scope:** Do not store damp garments in closed spaces; ask before reorganising other people’s belongings.

**Evidence to record:** Garment categories, folding method, retrieval ease and storage condition.

## MS-13-009 — Plan a simple balanced meal

**Target performance:** Plan a practical meal with varied food groups, sufficient portions and available ingredients without treating one template as a medical diet.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use familiar local foods and discuss dietary needs, allergies and household preferences with a responsible adult.

**Acquisition sequence:**

Include a source of protein, staple/energy food and fruit or vegetables where available; use local dietary guidance for variety. 3. Consider preparation time, storage and budget. 4. Check allergies, religious/cultural needs and household preferences. 5. Choose portions appropriate to appetite and age without calorie restriction or weight-loss goals. 6. Plan safe storage and any adult-supervised preparation. 7. Review what was eaten and what could be varied next time.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, review variety and practicality and adjust based on actual availability and preference.

**Transfer task:** Create a simple shopping list or substitute plan using nutritionally similar familiar foods.

**Safety and scope:** Not medical nutrition advice. For growth, illness, allergies or dietary restrictions, follow guardian and qualified health-professional guidance.

**Evidence to record:** Meal plan, ingredients, food-group rationale, allergy check and feasibility notes.

## MS-13-010 — Use a household checklist

**Target performance:** Use a short checklist to complete a recurring household task reliably while retaining awareness of exceptions.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a safe age-appropriate task approved by a responsible adult.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Define what “done” means for the task. 2. List only observable steps in the correct order. 3. Add a safety check where relevant. 4. Complete the task while checking each step. 5. At the end, inspect the result rather than trusting checked boxes alone. 6. Note any unusual condition that requires adult help. 7. Remove redundant steps after several successful uses. 8. Practise once without the checklist to see whether the sequence is understood.

**Practice dose:** Use the checklist for three repetitions of the same task; keep it short enough to scan.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Task meets its defined result on all three attempts and learner can explain at least one exception that requires stopping.

**Common failure modes:** Checklist too long; checking boxes without doing steps; using it where judgment is needed; following a step despite an unsafe condition.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, complete once without prompts and update only if an omission occurs.

**Transfer task:** Create a checklist for a different task and distinguish routine steps from exception handling.

**Safety and scope:** Do not perform tasks involving chemicals, heat, sharp tools or heights without adult approval and supervision.

**Evidence to record:** Checklist version, completed steps, result inspection, exception and independent attempt.

## MS-14-001 — Break a task into next actions

**Target performance:** Convert a vague goal into a sequence of small, observable actions with a clear first step and stopping condition.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a real low-risk task that can be completed in one sitting or a few sessions.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Write the desired outcome in one sentence. 2. Identify the current state and constraints. 3. List the major stages in outcome order. 4. Break the first stage into actions that can be started without another planning step. 5. Make the next action physically or digitally observable. 6. Estimate dependencies and identify what must happen first. 7. Begin the first action and revise the plan when new information appears.

**Practice dose:** Plan one task in 5–10 minutes, then start the first action rather than endlessly refining the plan.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, break down a new task and compare planned versus actual stages.

**Transfer task:** Use for a small coding feature, school assignment or household project.

**Safety and scope:** Do not use task decomposition to bypass safety checks or qualified approval.

**Evidence to record:** Goal, action list, dependencies, first action, completion evidence and revisions.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-14-002 — Estimate task duration realistically

**Target performance:** Estimate a task’s duration using comparable past work, uncertainty and interruption allowance, then compare the estimate with actual time.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a repeatable low-risk task; avoid using estimates to schedule sleep, meals or safety-critical work too tightly.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Define the task boundary and what counts as complete. 2. Break it into two or three stages. 3. Recall a comparable past task or perform a small sample. 4. Estimate active work time for each stage. 5. Add a separate allowance for setup, transitions and likely interruptions. 6. Record a range when uncertainty is high rather than a false exact number. 7. Complete the task and record actual time. 8. Identify whether the difference came from scope, skill, interruption or poor assumptions.

**Practice dose:** Track five ordinary tasks over time; do not time every activity or create pressure around rest.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** After several observations, estimates become more realistic and learner can explain the main source of error.

**Common failure modes:** Confusing elapsed time with focused time; ignoring setup; assuming best-case pace; treating a single overrun as failure.

**Delayed retention check:** Review estimates after one week and adjust based on repeated patterns.

**Transfer task:** Use ranges to plan a project milestone and communicate uncertainty honestly.

**Safety and scope:** Do not sacrifice sleep, meals, breaks or safety to meet an estimate.

**Evidence to record:** Task boundary, estimate/range, actual duration, interruption and error explanation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-14-003 — Create a simple contingency plan

**Target performance:** Identify likely disruptions and define safe fallback actions, triggers and communication for a small project or outing.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a low-risk plan with a manageable number of uncertainties.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. State the primary plan and its success condition. 2. Identify the two or three most plausible disruptions. 3. For each, define an observable trigger that means the plan should change. 4. Choose a safe alternative action and who should be informed. 5. Identify resources or information needed for the fallback. 6. Avoid planning for every imaginable event. 7. Review whether the fallback creates a new hazard or dependency. 8. Communicate the plan to relevant people before it is needed.

**Practice dose:** Create one contingency plan with no more than three scenarios; review when conditions change.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** For each scenario, learner can state trigger, safe next action and communication route.

**Common failure modes:** Vague fallback (“figure it out”); no trigger; unsafe shortcut; excessive overplanning; failing to tell affected people.

**Delayed retention check:** After the task, record which contingency was useful and revise the plan for next time.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a project deadline, transport disruption or device failure with different fallback needs.

**Safety and scope:** For emergencies, follow official guidance and trusted adult instructions; a personal contingency plan does not replace emergency services.

**Evidence to record:** Primary plan, scenarios, triggers, fallback actions, contacts and post-event review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-14-004 — Maintain a short decision log

**Delayed retention check:** Review after the trigger or in one month and add a dated outcome note rather than overwriting the entry.

**Transfer task:** Use for an architecture choice, purchase comparison or study-plan change.

**Safety and scope:** Keep personal and account-sensitive details out of shared logs; store files securely.

**Evidence to record:** Date, decision, options, criteria, evidence, assumptions, rationale and review outcome.

## MS-14-006 — Prioritise by consequence and urgency

**Target performance:** Rank tasks using impact, time sensitivity, dependencies and effort rather than treating every request as equally urgent.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a small list of 5–8 real tasks; exclude emergencies and medical/safety decisions from casual scoring.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Write each task with its real deadline and consequence of delay. 2. Identify tasks that block other work. 3. Separate urgent from important; a loud request is not automatically high priority. 4. Identify quick tasks only when they meaningfully reduce risk or unblock work. 5. Select a small number of next actions that fit available time and energy. 6. Communicate when a lower-priority task will be delayed. 7. Reassess when deadlines or consequences change.

**Delayed retention check:** At the end of the session, compare intended and actual priority order and explain deviations.

**Transfer task:** Apply to a multi-discipline study week by protecting foundational tasks and sustainable recovery.

**Safety and scope:** Do not use prioritisation to delay urgent safety/health needs; involve trusted adults when needed.

**Evidence to record:** Task list, criteria, chosen order, deferred items and review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-14-007 — Prepare a concise handoff

**Target performance:** Transfer work to another person with enough context, current state, next action, risks and file locations to continue without guessing.

State the next action and expected output. 5. Note blockers, assumptions and known risks. 6. Identify who owns the next step and any deadline already agreed. 7. **Transfer task:** Use for a Claude/ChatGPT project handoff with canonical files, decisions and exact open tasks.

**Safety and scope:** Remove credentials and private data; grant only necessary access.

**Evidence to record:** Handoff, recipient questions, missing details, artefact versions and successful continuation.

## MS-14-009 — Compare two options with a decision matrix

**Target performance:** Compare alternatives against explicit weighted criteria while recognising uncertainty and avoiding false numerical precision.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Choose a low-stakes decision with two or three plausible options and criteria that matter.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. State the decision and constraints. 2. Choose three to five criteria relevant to the outcome. 3. Assign simple weights only if some criteria genuinely matter more. 4. **Delayed retention check:** After the decision, compare outcome with assumptions and note what the matrix failed to capture.

**Transfer task:** Apply to selecting a learning resource, device accessory or project approach; keep safety and compatibility as hard constraints.

**Safety and scope:** Do not use a simple matrix as the sole basis for medical, legal, financial or safety-critical decisions.

**Evidence to record:** Options, criteria, weights, evidence, unknowns, sensitivity check and outcome.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.

## MS-15-011 — Faro Shuffle (introductory card interweave)

**Target performance:** Perform a basic controlled interweave of two equal card packets with minimal bending and clear alignment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use an inexpensive deck on a clean flat surface. Learn a standard beginner method from a reputable card-handling instructor.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Split the deck into two approximately equal packets. 2. Hold packets with relaxed, consistent grip and align inner corners. 3. Begin with a loose, shallow interweave rather than forcing a perfect weave. 4. Alternate cards gradually while maintaining packet alignment. 5. Square the deck gently without excessive bending. 6. Inspect for damaged corners or cards. 7. Practise the same direction repeatedly before attempting an in-faro/out-faro distinction.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete a visibly interwoven shuffle with no severe card bending; identify whether the weave is approximate or a true controlled faro.

**Common failure modes:** Forcing packets; using worn/slippery cards; confusing an imperfect riffle with a faro; practising too fast.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, repeat slowly and compare alignment and card damage.

**Transfer task:** Only after basic control, learn how a perfect faro differs structurally and why it requires precise packet size and alignment.

**Safety and scope:** Keep cards away from small children/pets; do not bend cards near face or use sharp tools to modify them.

**Evidence to record:** Deck type, packet balance, successful interweaves, card condition and delayed performance.
