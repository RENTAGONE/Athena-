# ATHENA Micro-skill Acquisition Protocols — Batch 01

**Scope:** First fully authored protocols for cross-disciplinary cognitive micro-skills. These are practice designs, not guarantees of a fixed acquisition time. The learner must pass independent and delayed checks before a skill can be labelled retained.

## Protocol standard

Every protocol separates (a) learning the procedure, (b) guided practice, (c) independent performance, (d) delayed cold retrieval, and (e) transfer to a changed context. A practice session is not evidence of retention merely because the learner completed it. A successful attempt must be recorded with conditions, prompts, quality, and assessor/evidence provenance.

---

## MS-01-001 — Active recall from a blank page

**Target:** Reconstruct a topic's key structure without notes, then identify and correct omissions.

**Prerequisites:** The topic has been studied at least once; the learner can identify its scope. No prerequisite to use this method on a simple familiar topic.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Select a bounded topic that can be represented on one page. State the question: “What are the main ideas and how do they connect?”
2. Close notes, tabs and source material. Set a short, non-punitive time limit appropriate to topic size.
3. Write or sketch everything retrievable: definitions, steps, examples, causes, relationships and uncertainties. Do not look up gaps during recall.
4. Reopen the source and compare line by line. Mark omissions, distortions and unsupported additions separately.
5. Correct the reconstruction in a different notation or explain the missing relationship aloud.
6. Create one follow-up prompt for each important gap. Do not simply copy the source as the correction.

**Common errors:** Looking at notes while “recalling”; writing familiar phrases without relationships; treating a blank as failure rather than diagnostic evidence; correcting by copying without a second retrieval attempt.

**Independent assessment:** Given a previously studied bounded topic, produce an accurate outline covering its essential concepts and relationships without source access. The assessor checks conceptual accuracy, coverage and invented claims, not handwriting or artistic layout.

**Delayed retention:** Repeat cold after a meaningful delay chosen by the scheduler. A pass requires the essential structure and relationships to remain accurate; minor wording differences are acceptable. If the topic has changed, record version/context.

**Transfer task:** Use blank-page recall to reconstruct a procedure, a scientific explanation and a business concept; compare what representation works best for each.

**Evidence:** Dated unaided artefact, source used for verification, error categories, delayed attempt and context.

## MS-01-002 — Confidence calibration

**Target:** Estimate confidence before feedback and improve the match between stated confidence and actual correctness over repeated low-stakes judgments.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Use a set of questions with answer keys or verifiable outcomes. Avoid high-stakes decisions.
2. Before checking each answer, record a confidence estimate (for example 50%, 70%, 90%) and a brief reason.
3. Mark correctness only after committing the answer and confidence.
4. Group results into confidence bands. Compare the proportion correct in each band; a small sample is descriptive, not conclusive.
5. Inspect overconfidence and underconfidence separately. Identify whether errors came from missing knowledge, misread wording, weak evidence or unjustified certainty.
6. On a later set, use a deliberate “what would change my mind?” check before assigning confidence.

**Common errors:** Changing confidence after seeing the answer; treating a tiny sample as proof of calibration; confusing confidence with competence; using confidence scores to shame the learner.

**Independent assessment:** Complete a fresh mixed set, record confidence before feedback and produce a short calibration reflection. Success means the process is followed honestly and the learner can identify a pattern; immediate numerical improvement is not required from a small sample.

**Delayed retention:** Repeat with a different topic and compare multiple sessions, not a single score.

**Transfer task:** Apply confidence calibration to a low-stakes prediction and later compare prediction with outcome.

**Evidence:** Question set, pre-feedback confidence, correctness, sample size, reflection and later comparison.

## MS-01-003 — Explain a concept simply

**Target:** Explain a concept in plain language without losing its defining mechanism, conditions or limits.

**Practice protocol:**
1. State the concept in one precise sentence.
2. Identify the mechanism or relationship that makes the concept work; do not begin with an analogy.
3. Explain it to an imagined beginner using familiar words, then define any unavoidable technical term.
4. Give one concrete example and one non-example or boundary case.
5. Ask “why?”, “how?” and “when would this not apply?” and answer without notes.
6. Compare the explanation with a reliable reference or subject expert; correct simplifications that became false.

**Common errors:** Removing essential conditions; using a metaphor as if it were the mechanism; replacing explanation with a list of synonyms; oversimplifying until the statement is inaccurate.

**Independent assessment:** Deliver a 60–90 second explanation. A listener should be able to paraphrase the central mechanism and answer two basic application questions. Technical correctness is more important than performance style.

**Delayed retention:** Explain again after a delay to a different audience or in a written format.

**Transfer task:** Explain the same underlying concept in a new context and identify which parts of the explanation remain invariant.

**Evidence:** Recording or transcript, listener paraphrase, follow-up questions, correction notes and delayed explanation.

## MS-01-004 — Build a useful analogy

**Target:** Map a difficult concept to a familiar system while explicitly identifying where the analogy stops working.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Write the target concept's actual components and relationships first.
2. Choose a familiar source system with a similar structure, not merely a similar appearance.
3. Create a mapping table: target element → source element → relationship preserved.
4. Test the analogy against at least one ordinary case and one edge case.
5. Write at least two limits: a place where the source system differs or would produce a false inference.
6. Replace or qualify any mapping that fails; if no analogy is safe, use a direct explanation instead.

**Common errors:** Forcing every detail to match; confusing illustration with evidence; hiding the analogy's limits; selecting a catchy but structurally unrelated comparison.

**Independent assessment:** Produce an analogy with at least three valid correspondences and two explicit limits. A reviewer checks that the analogy does not introduce a false causal claim.

**Delayed retention:** Reconstruct the mapping and limits without the original notes.

**Transfer task:** Use an analogy to teach a concept to a novice, then identify which parts must be explained literally.

**Evidence:** Mapping table, edge-case test, limits and revised version.

## MS-01-005 — Spot a hidden assumption

**Target:** Identify an unstated premise on which a claim or argument depends.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Rewrite the claim as a conclusion that could be true or false.
2. List the explicit evidence and reasons offered.
3. Ask what must also be true for those reasons to support the conclusion.
4. Generate at least two plausible alternative explanations or conditions.
5. Test whether the conclusion still follows if the assumption is false.
6. State what evidence could check the assumption.

**Common errors:** Inventing irrelevant assumptions; confusing a missing fact with a premise; assuming every unstated premise is malicious; attacking the speaker rather than the argument.

**Independent assessment:** Analyse five short arguments and identify a relevant hidden assumption for at least four, with a reason showing why it matters.

**Delayed retention:** Repeat using unfamiliar topics so the learner cannot rely on memorised examples.

**Transfer task:** Apply the method to a product claim, a scientific headline and an everyday plan.

**Evidence:** Original argument, reconstructed premise, counterfactual test and evidence proposal.

## MS-01-006 — Separate fact, inference and opinion

**Target:** Distinguish directly supported observations, conclusions drawn from them, and value judgments or preferences.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Break a paragraph into individual claims; do not classify a whole paragraph as one unit.
2. Label direct observations or verifiable reports as factual claims, while recording the source needed to verify them.
3. Label conclusions that go beyond the observation as inferences and state the reasoning bridge.
4. Label preferences or value judgments as opinions; identify any factual premises they rely on.
5. Mark mixed claims and split them into components.
6. Identify which claims need external verification and what would count as adequate evidence.

**Common errors:** Treating “fact” as “something I believe”; assuming opinions have no factual premises; treating a source's statement as independently verified; missing mixed claims.

**Independent assessment:** Classify 15 claims, justify ambiguous cases and flag unsupported factual claims. Success requires defensible reasoning, not merely matching a label key.

**Delayed retention:** Apply to a new article or conversation transcript after a delay.

**Transfer task:** Annotate a short advertisement or social-media post and separate observable evidence from inference and persuasion.

**Evidence:** Annotated claims, source checks and explanation of ambiguous classifications.

## MS-01-007 — Create a retrieval prompt

**Target:** Convert a learning objective into questions that require useful retrieval rather than recognition alone.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Start with an observable objective and identify what a learner must actually retrieve or do.
2. Write one free-recall prompt that requires generation without options.
3. Write one explanation prompt asking why or how.
4. Write one application prompt with a concrete new case.
5. Write one discrimination prompt requiring the learner to distinguish similar concepts or methods.
6. Write one transfer prompt in a changed context; include an answer criterion or rubric.
7. Test the prompts: remove the notes and check whether each prompt elicits the intended knowledge.

**Common errors:** Writing vague prompts (“explain everything”); making every prompt definition recall; embedding the answer in the wording; omitting success criteria.

**Independent assessment:** Create five prompts spanning recall, explanation, application, discrimination and transfer, with answer criteria. A reviewer checks alignment to the objective.

**Delayed retention:** Rebuild a prompt set for a different subject without consulting the original examples.

**Transfer task:** Convert one practical skill objective into an observable performance test rather than a text-only quiz.

**Evidence:** Objective, prompt set, answer criteria and revision after trial.

## MS-01-008 — Error log analysis

**Target:** Record errors, diagnose likely causes and select a corrective practice that tests the diagnosis.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Record the task, expected result and actual result without blame language.
2. Classify the likely error source: knowledge gap, concept misunderstanding, strategy choice, procedure, attention, tool/environment or communication.
3. Identify evidence for the diagnosis and note uncertainty if multiple causes are plausible.
4. Select one targeted correction, not a broad instruction to “try harder”.
5. Complete a fresh parallel task to test whether the correction works.
6. If the error repeats, revise the diagnosis rather than endlessly repeating the same drill.

**Common errors:** Logging only the wrong answer; labelling all errors as carelessness; changing several variables at once; treating one successful retry as proof the issue is solved.

**Independent assessment:** Analyse three errors, distinguish plausible causes and propose a testable corrective practice for each.

**Delayed retention:** Review an older error and predict whether the original diagnosis still fits; test with a new task.

**Transfer task:** Use the method on a coding bug, a mathematics error and a communication breakdown.

**Evidence:** Error record, diagnosis, confidence in diagnosis, corrective task and outcome.

## MS-01-009 — Estimate before calculating

**Target:** Generate a rough expected magnitude before exact calculation and use it to detect implausible results.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Identify the quantity, units and expected scale.
2. Round values or use a simple benchmark that preserves the order of magnitude.
3. Estimate mentally or with a rough model before using a calculator.
4. Perform the exact calculation independently.
5. Compare estimate and result; investigate differences larger than the task warrants.
6. State whether the estimate is a bound, approximation or central guess.

**Common errors:** Rounding in a way that changes scale; estimating after seeing the answer; treating an estimate as exact; ignoring units.

**Independent assessment:** Solve a mixed set of numerical problems, writing an estimate and unit before each exact result. Success requires plausible scale and a reasonableness check.

**Delayed retention:** Repeat with unfamiliar quantities and at least one multi-step problem.

**Transfer task:** Estimate a project cost, travel time or storage requirement before calculating precisely.

**Evidence:** Pre-calculation estimate, exact result, units, discrepancy explanation and delayed check.

## MS-01-010 — Break a complex task into steps

**Target:** Decompose a goal into ordered, testable sub-tasks with dependencies and a clear completion condition.

**Practice protocol:**
1. Define the final deliverable and how someone will verify it is done.
2. Identify major phases and their outputs.
3. Break each phase into actions small enough to start and verify.
4. Mark dependencies, decision points, required resources and likely failure points.
5. Identify the first action and a safe stopping point.
6. Run the plan on a small version of the task; revise steps that were ambiguous or missing.

**Common errors:** Decomposing into vague labels; making a long list without dependencies; confusing activity with output; planning every detail before testing.

**Independent assessment:** Decompose a new moderate task into actionable steps with dependencies, deliverables and a definition of done. A second person should be able to follow the plan without guessing.

**Delayed retention:** Decompose a different task after a delay and compare whether hidden dependencies are detected.

**Transfer task:** Apply decomposition to a small coding feature, a school project and a household organisation task.

**Evidence:** Task definition, step map, dependency notes, trial result and revised plan.
