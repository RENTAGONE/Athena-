# Micro-skill Protocol — Kitchen Mise en Place (Message 18)

**Stable candidate ID:** `MS-13-011`  
**Status:** Substantive internal draft; subject review required  
**Scope:** Safe preparation and organisation of a kitchen station before a simple recipe. This is not a food-safety certification.

## Target performance
Before beginning a simple supervised recipe, identify ingredients and tools, check quantities, prepare ingredients safely, arrange the workspace in task order, keep raw and ready-to-eat foods separated, and restore the station.

## Entry conditions and boundaries
- Begin with a no-heat recipe or a recipe approved by a trusted adult.
- A trusted adult handles heat, sharp knives, appliances and any task beyond the learner's demonstrated ability.
- Check allergies and ingredient identity before handling or serving food.
- Wash hands, clean the work surface and use separate clean utensils/boards where raw foods require separation.
- Do not use unidentified ingredients, taste raw risky foods, or improvise food storage temperatures.

## Practice protocol
1. Read the recipe once without starting. Identify the final result and the order of major steps.
2. List each ingredient with the quantity and each tool/container needed.
3. Check what is actually available. Mark missing, substituted or uncertain items rather than silently improvising.
4. Wash hands and prepare a clean, uncluttered work surface.
5. Arrange ingredients in the order they will be used. Keep raw animal products separate from ready-to-eat foods and clean items.
6. Prepare only age-appropriate ingredients. Ask for direct adult help for knives, heat, appliances or unfamiliar tools.
7. Before starting, perform a station check: ingredients, quantities, tools, hygiene, separation, safe equipment and clear workspace.
8. After the task, store ingredients according to their labels and local food-safety guidance, clean the station and record one improvement.

## Worked example
A no-heat fruit-and-yoghurt bowl needs a clean bowl, spoon, yoghurt, washed fruit and a clean preparation surface. The station plan places the bowl and spoon first, checks the yoghurt date/storage label, washes the fruit, keeps the clean bowl away from unwashed produce, and puts chilled yoghurt back promptly. If the yoghurt's storage history is uncertain, do not use it; ask an adult. The plan is not merely a shopping list: it prepares the work area and sequence so the task can be performed safely and without avoidable interruption.

## Practice and evidence
- **Guided:** Prepare a station checklist for a familiar no-heat snack; have an adult check allergy, hygiene and storage assumptions.
- **Independent:** For a different simple recipe, create the checklist and arrange the station before beginning.
- **Evidence:** checklist, photo or observer note of the arranged station (avoid identifiable people), missing-item/error note, and delayed recall of the sequence on another day.

## Success criteria
- All required ingredients and tools are identified; quantities are checked against the recipe.
- The work surface and hand-hygiene steps are prepared before food handling.
- Ingredients are arranged in task order and raw/ready-to-eat separation is maintained where relevant.
- The learner identifies an uncertainty or missing item and pauses/escalates instead of guessing.
- The station is restored and perishable ingredients are stored appropriately.

## Common misconceptions
- Mise en place is not only a shopping list; it includes preparing and arranging the station.
- A tidy layout does not compensate for unsafe storage, cross-contamination or allergy oversight.
- Recipe substitutions are not automatically equivalent; allergens, cooking requirements and food safety may change.

## Assessment and retry
Use a different simple recipe. Score each criterion as not yet / developing / demonstrated. Give feedback on the first unmet criterion, repeat the station plan and then assess with a changed recipe. A checklist or photo alone does not establish independent practical competence.

## Retention and transfer
Use short distributed practice during ordinary, supervised food preparation. After a delay, ask the learner to reconstruct the station plan for a new recipe and identify one safety or sequencing risk. Increase spacing only after consistent safe performance.

## Review requirements
Qualified food-safety review, age-appropriate usability review and learner pilot remain outstanding. This protocol does not replace local food-safety rules or adult supervision.
