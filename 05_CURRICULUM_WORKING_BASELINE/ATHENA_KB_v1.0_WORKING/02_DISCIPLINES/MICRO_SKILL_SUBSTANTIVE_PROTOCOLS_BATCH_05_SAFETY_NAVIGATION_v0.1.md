# ATHENA Substantive Micro-skill Protocols — Batch 05

**Status:** Substantive authored drafts; not independently reviewed, calibrated or certified.

**Scope:** 20 safety-awareness and navigation protocols. CPR/AED and choking-response entries are explicitly training-pathway records, not technique instructions or certification.


## MS-07-001 — Identify local emergency contact route

**Target performance:** Know how to reach the appropriate local emergency service or trusted adult and communicate the location and nature of an emergency.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use official local government/operator information and verify it with a trusted adult. Do not place test calls to emergency numbers.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the country and locality where the learner normally spends time. 2. Find the official emergency number and any relevant service-specific route from a government or recognised emergency-service source. 3. Record the number in an approved, accessible place without publishing it. 4. Identify a trusted adult and a backup contact. 5. Practise a fictional scenario: say who needs help, exact location, callback number if appropriate, what happened and immediate hazards. 6. Explain when to call emergency services rather than relying on social media or an unverified contact. 7. Check whether the route differs when travelling or using a mobile network.

**Practice dose:** Review once with a trusted adult, then rehearse a fictional scenario twice per year or when location/service details change.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner can identify the verified local route, state the location clearly and name a trusted adult backup without making a live test call.

**Common failure modes:** Relying on an outdated number; giving vague location; assuming a messaging app is an emergency service; delaying a genuine emergency to search online.

**Delayed retention check:** Reconfirm the official source after moving or when service information changes; practise the scenario after several months.

**Transfer task:** Create a small emergency contact card for a family-approved setting and explain which details should remain private.

**Safety and scope:** Never make prank/test calls. In a real emergency, follow dispatcher instructions and involve a trusted adult where possible.

**Evidence to record:** Official source/date, verified contact route, fictional scenario response and adult confirmation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-002 — Check a scene for immediate hazards

**Target performance:** Pause before approaching an incident, identify obvious hazards from a safe distance and avoid becoming another casualty.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a tabletop scenario or instructor-led simulation; do not approach real hazards for practice.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Stop at a safe distance and look for traffic, fire/smoke, electricity, unstable structures, violence, chemicals, water or other ongoing danger. 2. Do not touch a person or object if the scene may be electrically or chemically hazardous. 3. Decide whether it is safe to remain, move back or leave. 4. Alert emergency services or a responsible adult when danger is present. 5. Give a concise description of hazards and location. 6. Only approach when the scene is confirmed safe and within your training and role. 7. Reassess if conditions change.

**Practice dose:** Use three instructor-provided scenarios; verbalise “stop, look, keep distance, call, reassess”.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identifies the primary hazard and chooses not to enter an unsafe scene in all scenarios; explains why rescuer safety comes first.

**Common failure modes:** Rushing to help; assuming a hazard has ended; touching downed wires or unknown liquids; entering traffic or smoke.

**Delayed retention check:** After one month, work through a new scenario and explain what information would change the safety decision.

**Transfer task:** Apply the same pause-and-assess model to a workshop spill or roadside incident without attempting untrained rescue.

**Safety and scope:** This is awareness training, not rescue certification. Do not enter fire, traffic, electrical, chemical, confined-space or violent scenes. Follow trained responders.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario, hazards identified, safe-distance decision, call/escalation choice and rationale.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-003 — Communicate an emergency clearly

**Target performance:** Transmit essential emergency information in a calm, ordered way and answer dispatcher questions without guessing.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Practise with fictional scenarios and a trusted adult. Use the locally verified emergency route only in real emergencies.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. State that an emergency is occurring and what type of help is needed. 2. Give the exact location first, including building/road/landmark and access details. 3. Describe what happened and the number of people affected if known. 4. State immediate hazards and whether responders can approach safely. 5. Give a callback number if requested and safe. 6. Answer questions directly; say “I don’t know” rather than inventing details. 7. Do not hang up until instructed. 8. Follow dispatcher instructions and update them if the situation changes.

**Practice dose:** Rehearse two fictional scenarios with an adult; keep the practice under five minutes.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** A listener can write down the location, incident type, hazards and requested response from the learner’s report without needing repeated clarification.

**Common failure modes:** Starting with a long story; omitting location; exaggerating injuries; guessing age/condition; ending the call early.

**Delayed retention check:** After several weeks, repeat with a different scenario and compare completeness.

**Transfer task:** Use the structure to report a non-emergency hazard to a school or building supervisor.

**Safety and scope:** Do not share emergency-call recordings or another person’s medical details publicly. Never make a false emergency report.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario script, information completeness, clarity, unknowns correctly stated and adult feedback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-004 — Basic digital account recovery hygiene

**Target performance:** Use official recovery routes and protect recovery codes, sessions and identity information during account recovery.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a mock account or review settings without triggering a real recovery flow. A trusted adult should supervise accounts under family management.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the official app/site already used for the account; do not follow recovery links from unsolicited messages. 2. Confirm the domain or app publisher through a known route. 3. Check recovery email/phone ownership and update outdated methods through official settings. 4. Store recovery codes in a secure password manager or approved offline location. 5. Sign out unknown sessions only after understanding the effect and ensuring access to the account. 6. Enable available multifactor protection. 7. If locked out, use the official recovery process and document the case number without sharing codes. 8. Warn contacts if the account may be compromised.

**Practice dose:** Review one account’s recovery settings with an adult; do not generate or disclose actual recovery codes in practice evidence.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner can identify official recovery path, explain why codes are secret, and describe the first safe steps after suspected compromise.

**Common failure modes:** Sharing one-time codes; trusting support DMs; paying “recovery agents”; deleting evidence before securing account; storing codes in screenshots.

**Delayed retention check:** Recheck recovery methods after one month and after changing phone/email access.

**Transfer task:** Apply the checklist to a second mock service and compare recovery options without revealing secrets.

**Safety and scope:** Never disclose passwords, OTPs, backup codes or recovery phrases to anyone, including this assistant. Seek a trusted adult if compromised.

**Evidence to record:** Settings checklist with secrets omitted, official route, MFA status and review date.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-005 — Recognise unsafe electrical conditions

**Target performance:** Recognise common signs of electrical danger and avoid contact, isolation attempts or repairs beyond one’s competence.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use photographs or instructor-provided scenarios only; do not inspect damaged live equipment.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Recognise warning signs: exposed conductors, damaged insulation, burning smell, heat, sparks, buzzing, water near electrical equipment or repeated breaker trips. 2. Stop using the affected equipment and keep others away. 3. Do not touch the equipment, plug, wire or a person in contact with electricity. 4. Keep clear of water and metal objects. 5. Tell a responsible adult and arrange qualified electrical help. 6. If a person may be receiving an electric shock, do not touch them until the power source is safely isolated by a competent person; call emergency services. 7. Treat overhead/downed power lines as live and stay well away.

**Practice dose:** Classify 10 scenario images; practise the safe response verbally. No hands-on electrical fault simulation.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identifies all high-risk scenarios and selects “keep away, warn, notify adult/qualified responder” rather than touching or repairing.

**Common failure modes:** Assuming a device is safe because it is switched off; using water; touching a shock victim; approaching downed wires; resetting a breaker repeatedly.

**Delayed retention check:** After one month, classify a fresh set and explain why the hazard may persist after visible activity stops.

**Transfer task:** Use the recognition model when evaluating a workshop or household setup before any practical electronics activity.

**Safety and scope:** Awareness only. Never open mains equipment, handle exposed wiring or perform electrical repairs. All mains work belongs to qualified professionals.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario, hazard sign, safe-distance decision and escalation action.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-006 — Read a basic product safety label

**Target performance:** Extract the key hazards, required precautions, storage/disposal instructions and age restrictions from a product label.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use an ordinary household product in its closed container; do not open, smell, mix or test it.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify product name and intended use. 2. Locate hazard pictograms and signal words. 3. Read precautionary statements and first-aid directions without attempting a test. 4. Check age restrictions, protective equipment and ventilation requirements. 5. Identify storage incompatibilities and keep-away instructions. 6. Read disposal guidance and expiry/use-by information where relevant. 7. If the label is damaged or unreadable, do not use the product until an adult verifies it. 8. Explain one thing the label does not authorise you to do.

**Practice dose:** Read three labels from sealed products with an adult; compare hazard categories.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identify the main hazard, one required precaution, storage condition and what to do if label information is missing.

**Common failure modes:** Reading only the front marketing panel; ignoring pictograms; assuming dilution makes a chemical harmless; mixing products.

**Delayed retention check:** After a few weeks, read a different label and identify which instructions are product-specific.

**Transfer task:** Apply the same approach to batteries, chargers or tools, distinguishing chemical warnings from electrical/mechanical warnings.

**Safety and scope:** Do not open, mix, ingest or deliberately smell products. Follow guardian instructions; urgent exposure requires adult/emergency/poison-control guidance appropriate locally.

**Evidence to record:** Product category, label symbols, precautions, storage/disposal note and adult confirmation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-007 — Use a fire extinguisher decision model

**Target performance:** Recognise when evacuation and emergency calling are safer than attempting extinguisher use; understand the limits of basic extinguisher awareness.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use instructor material or a certified fire-safety demonstration. No live flame practice.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Prioritise alerting others, activating the alarm and evacuating. 2. Identify whether the fire is growing, producing heavy smoke, blocking exits or involving unknown materials; if so, leave immediately. 3. Never fight a fire if you lack training, the correct extinguisher, a clear escape route or adult/authority permission. 4. Understand that extinguishers are class/rating-specific; the wrong agent can worsen a fire. 5. If trained and authorised, follow the extinguisher label and local fire-safety instruction while keeping an exit behind you. 6. Stop immediately if the fire does not go out or conditions change. 7. Close doors on the way out if safe; never re-enter.

**Practice dose:** Review a certified fire-safety video or attend an instructor-led session; practise decisions on paper only.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** In all scenarios, learner chooses evacuation when conditions exceed safe limits and can state that extinguisher type and training matter.

**Common failure modes:** Treating an extinguisher as universal; approaching smoke; blocking own exit; re-entering for belongings; assuming a small fire will stay small.

**Delayed retention check:** Review the decision model annually or when the building’s fire plan changes; practical skill requires qualified instruction.

**Transfer task:** Explain the building’s evacuation route and assembly point to a family member or supervisor.

**Safety and scope:** No flame, smoke or extinguisher discharge practice outside qualified supervised training. In a real fire, evacuate and call emergency services.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario decisions, evacuation route knowledge, training source and review date.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-008 — Basic wound-care literacy

**Target performance:** Recognise when a minor wound needs adult help and understand clean, conservative first-aid principles without attempting advanced treatment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use diagrams or a first-aid course; no practice on an actual injured person.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify whether bleeding is severe, spurting, persistent, associated with a serious object or accompanied by faintness or major injury. 2. For serious bleeding or major injury, call emergency services and follow dispatcher instructions; do not delay. 3. For a minor superficial wound, involve a responsible adult, use clean hands and appropriate first-aid supplies, and follow recognised local first-aid guidance. 4. Do not remove deeply embedded objects. 5. Watch for infection signs or worsening pain and seek medical advice. 6. Check tetanus or other immunisation concerns with a qualified health professional when relevant. 7. Dispose of contaminated materials safely.

**Practice dose:** Study a recognised first-aid source and complete a supervised course if available; this literacy protocol is not practical certification.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Learner distinguishes minor from potentially serious injury and consistently escalates red-flag cases rather than improvising treatment.

**Common failure modes:** Applying unverified substances; removing embedded objects; ignoring severe bleeding; using dirty materials; treating online advice as diagnosis.

**Delayed retention check:** Review from a recognised first-aid provider when guidance changes and after any actual incident with a responsible adult.

**Transfer task:** Explain the difference between a minor scrape scenario and a serious injury scenario, including when professional care is needed.

**Safety and scope:** Not medical training. Serious bleeding, deep wounds, animal/human bites, eye injuries, burns, suspected fracture or deterioration require adult/emergency/clinical help.

**Evidence to record:** Source and date, scenario classification, escalation decision and course completion if any.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-009 — CPR/AED certified practical competency pathway

**Target performance:** Progress toward current, recognised CPR/AED competency through a qualified instructor and formal practical assessment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** This is a training pathway only. The protocol does not teach or certify CPR technique. Find a recognised local course with hands-on manikin practice and an instructor.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify a reputable provider and confirm the course covers the learner’s intended population and local emergency system. 2. Complete the provider’s prerequisites and guardian consent where required. 3. Attend instructor-led training covering recognition, emergency activation, CPR and AED use. 4. Practise on manikins and training AEDs under direct supervision. 5. Complete the provider’s practical assessment; ask for remediation on any failed component. 6. Record provider, course date, scope, certificate expiry or recommended renewal interval. 7. Follow the provider’s current renewal guidance; do not rely on this document as a substitute.

**Practice dose:** Follow the provider’s course duration and practice schedule. Renewal is determined by the certifying organisation and local guidance, not an ATHENA-invented interval.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Pass the provider’s current hands-on assessment and receive documented certification where offered. Reading this protocol alone never counts as competency.

**Common failure modes:** Learning from text/video alone; assuming adult and child protocols are identical; using an expired certificate as current; practising compressions on a person.

**Delayed retention check:** Renew and refresh according to the issuing organisation’s current guidance; keep prior certificates as historical evidence.

**Transfer task:** After certification, identify how to summon local emergency help and where approved AEDs are located in familiar public settings.

**Safety and scope:** Strictly instructor-led. Never practise compressions on a person. In a real emergency call local emergency services and follow dispatcher instructions; use an AED only as directed and within training.

**Evidence to record:** Provider, instructor, course scope, practical assessment outcome, certificate/expiry and renewal guidance.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-07-010 — Choking Response certified practical competency pathway

**Target performance:** Progress toward recognised choking-response competency through qualified instruction, age-specific guidance and practical assessment.

**Prerequisites and setup:** This is a pathway to certified training, not a standalone treatment guide. Select a recognised first-aid provider and confirm the course covers the relevant age groups.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify a qualified provider and course scope. 2. Confirm whether instruction covers adult, child and infant distinctions; do not assume techniques transfer between groups. 3. Attend supervised training using appropriate manikins. 4. Learn recognition, emergency activation and the provider’s current response sequence. 5. Practise only on training manikins under instructor supervision. 6. Complete the provider’s practical assessment. 7. Record course scope, date and renewal advice. 8. If an actual event occurs, call emergency services and follow dispatcher instructions.

**Practice dose:** Use the provider’s schedule and practice requirements; this document does not prescribe a technique or renewal interval.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Pass the provider’s practical assessment for the specified age group(s). Reading this protocol does not count as competency.

**Common failure modes:** Using one age group’s technique for another; relying on an outdated video; practising on a person; delaying emergency activation.

**Delayed retention check:** Refresh according to the certifying organisation and current local guidance; preserve prior evidence.

**Transfer task:** Explain how to summon help and identify the limits of one’s training without demonstrating a technique outside class.

**Safety and scope:** Strictly qualified instruction. Never practise thrusts or other manoeuvres on a person. In an emergency, activate local emergency response and follow dispatcher instructions.

**Evidence to record:** Provider, course scope, age groups, practical assessment, certificate and renewal recommendation.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-001 — Orient a map to the environment

**Target performance:** Align a map with the surrounding landscape using visible landmarks and distinguish map direction from the learner’s facing direction.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a safe, familiar open area and a paper map or offline map display; practise with a trusted adult.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the map’s north indicator and scale/legend. 2. Choose two visible landmarks that also appear on the map. 3. Rotate the map until mapped features align with their real-world directions. 4. Check a third landmark to reduce the chance of a false alignment. 5. Identify your approximate position and facing direction. 6. Reorient after moving or turning; do not assume the map remains aligned in your hand. 7. Compare with a compass or GPS only as a cross-check, noting that devices can be inaccurate.

**Practice dose:** Practise in two safe locations with several visible landmarks; keep the map accessible and avoid distraction.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly orient the map and identify at least three corresponding landmarks in two settings.

**Common failure modes:** Using only one landmark; confusing north-up with direction-of-travel; assuming GPS is exact; rotating the map but not updating position.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, orient a different map in a new safe place and explain any uncertainty.

**Transfer task:** Use orientation to plan a short supervised route and identify a landmark that confirms progress.

**Safety and scope:** Do not practise near roads, water edges or isolated areas. Follow guardian guidance and keep a charged phone when travelling.

**Evidence to record:** Map/source, landmarks used, estimated position, orientation result and cross-check.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-002 — Follow a multi-step route plan

**Target performance:** Follow a route in ordered segments while checking progress at landmarks and adapting safely when conditions differ.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Plan a short, familiar route with a trusted adult. Verify current route and service information before travel.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Divide the route into short legs between recognisable landmarks. 2. Record the next turn or stop and a confirmation cue for each leg. 3. Check direction before leaving each landmark. 4. At each leg’s end, confirm the expected landmark before continuing. 5. If the cue is absent, stop in a safe public place and re-check rather than guessing. 6. Use official signs or transit information where available. 7. Keep a fallback route and trusted contact method. 8. Report arrival according to the agreed check-in plan.

**Practice dose:** Walk through a simulated route on a map first; real route practice only with appropriate supervision.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Complete a supervised route with no unsafe shortcut and correctly identify at least four route checkpoints.

**Common failure modes:** Trying to recover while still moving; following a stranger; relying on memory after a missed turn; ignoring closures.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, describe the route from memory and identify where the plan would need updating.

**Transfer task:** Create a route plan for a different familiar destination, including a safe fallback and check-in.

**Safety and scope:** Do not travel alone for practice. Avoid isolated shortcuts; follow local rules and trusted adult instructions.

**Evidence to record:** Route legs, landmarks, confirmation cues, deviations, fallback and arrival check-in.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-003 — Estimate walking time and distance

**Target performance:** Produce a realistic approximate walking-time estimate using route length, terrain, crossings, crowding and personal pace.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a familiar, safe route with a map scale or measured distance. Do not use estimates to justify rushing.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Estimate route distance from a map or known segment. 2. Identify factors that slow travel: stairs, crossings, crowds, weather, carrying load and accessibility needs. 3. Use a measured personal walking pace from an ordinary safe walk, not a sprint. 4. Calculate a rough base time from distance and pace. 5. Add realistic allowance for crossings, navigation and delays. 6. Compare estimate with actual elapsed time after the walk. 7. Adjust future estimates from several observations rather than one trip.

**Practice dose:** Record three ordinary walks of known approximate distance; do not time-trial or rush.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Across three routes, estimates are within a reasonable margin (for example 20–30%) after accounting for conditions; learner explains sources of error.

**Common failure modes:** Using running pace; ignoring crossings; assuming map distance equals walking path; treating one observation as universal.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat an estimate after one week for a different route and compare with actual time.

**Transfer task:** Apply to transit interchange planning and include a buffer rather than aiming to arrive at the last possible minute.

**Safety and scope:** Never hurry across roads or sacrifice safety to meet an estimate. Account for weather, fatigue and accessibility.

**Evidence to record:** Distance source, elapsed time, conditions, estimate error and adjustment rationale.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-004 — Use a compass bearing safely

**Target performance:** Understand basic compass orientation and follow a simple bearing in a controlled, open environment without entering hazardous terrain.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a basic compass in a supervised open area away from large metal objects, vehicles and strong magnetic interference.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify the direction-of-travel arrow, magnetic needle and bearing scale on the specific compass. 2. Hold it level and allow the needle to settle. 3. Rotate the housing to the instructed bearing using the compass model’s markings. 4. Turn your body until the needle aligns with the orienting arrow as taught for that compass. 5. Select a visible safe landmark along the bearing rather than walking while staring at the compass. 6. Walk to the landmark, stop and re-check. 7. Note magnetic interference and local declination only when the map/route requires it and the learner has been taught how.

**Practice dose:** Practise with a short instructor-set bearing over open level ground; no wilderness navigation exercise.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identify compass parts and follow a short supervised bearing to a visible landmark, stopping to re-check safely.

**Common failure modes:** Holding compass tilted; confusing bearing with heading; walking while looking down; using compass near metal or magnets; ignoring local declination when precision matters.

**Delayed retention check:** Repeat with a new bearing after several days under supervision.

**Transfer task:** Explain when a compass is useful as a backup and why it should be combined with map, landmarks and route planning.

**Safety and scope:** Do not practise alone, near roads/water/cliffs or in remote areas. This is not a survival qualification.

**Evidence to record:** Compass model, bearing, environment, landmark, deviation and instructor feedback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-005 — Recognise orientation landmarks

**Target performance:** Select stable, distinctive landmarks that help confirm location and direction during a route.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a familiar supervised route; choose public, durable features such as named buildings, bridges or signed junctions.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Identify landmarks visible from the route and represented on the map. 2. Prefer distinctive and stable features over temporary signs, parked vehicles or advertisements. 3. Note the sequence in which landmarks should appear. 4. Identify one feature that could be confused with another and find a second confirmation cue. 5. Check the landmark from a safe stopping point. 6. Update the route description if construction or closures alter visibility.

**Practice dose:** Choose five landmarks on one familiar route and explain why each is reliable.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly match at least four of five landmarks and identify a second cue for the most ambiguous one.

**Common failure modes:** Using temporary objects; assuming a landmark proves exact position; ignoring direction of approach; relying on one cue only.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, recognise the same route landmarks from the opposite direction and note which are less useful.

**Transfer task:** Build a short written route guide for a family member using landmarks and clear turn instructions.

**Safety and scope:** Do not stop in roadways or unsafe locations to inspect landmarks. Avoid identifying private homes as public navigation targets.

**Evidence to record:** Landmark list, map match, stability rationale, ambiguity note and reverse-direction check.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-006 — Recover calmly after a wrong turn

**Target performance:** Respond to route uncertainty by stopping safely, checking reliable information and choosing a deliberate recovery action.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a tabletop map scenario or a supervised familiar route; do not intentionally become lost.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Notice the mismatch between expected and observed landmarks. 2. Stop in a safe, public place away from traffic and hazards. 3. Avoid continuing on a guessed route. 4. Recheck current position using at least two cues: map, official sign, landmark or trusted navigation device. 5. If still uncertain, contact the agreed trusted person or ask an appropriate official source for help. 6. Choose the safest known route back or wait in a safe place as advised. 7. Update the check-in contact if plans change.

**Practice dose:** Work through three fictional wrong-turn scenarios; rehearse the stop-and-check sequence.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** In every scenario, learner stops safely, avoids unsafe shortcuts and selects a verifiable next action.

**Common failure modes:** Panic-running; following an unknown person to an isolated place; hiding the mistake; continuing to guess; letting phone battery reach zero.

**Delayed retention check:** After a week, solve a new scenario and explain what information would justify moving again.

**Transfer task:** Create a route plan with a preselected safe fallback point and check-in procedure.

**Safety and scope:** For minors, follow guardian-approved travel rules. If threatened or in immediate danger, move toward staffed public help and contact emergency services.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario, first safe stopping choice, verification cues, contact decision and route recovery.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-007 — Give a precise location description

**Target performance:** Describe a location so another person can identify the correct place and access point without relying on vague directions.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a public, non-sensitive location such as a library, park entrance or transit stop.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. State the official place name if known. 2. Give the city/area and nearest named street, junction or public landmark. 3. Specify entrance, floor, platform or side only when verified. 4. Add a map pin or coordinates only if the source is reliable and sharing is appropriate. 5. Describe a visible confirmation feature. 6. Distinguish your current position from the destination. 7. Ask the listener to repeat the location if the situation is important.

**Practice dose:** Describe three public places to a partner who has not seen your map.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Partner can identify the intended place and access point without more than one clarification; no private address is disclosed.

**Common failure modes:** Saying “near the big shop”; confusing destination with current location; giving outdated entrance information; over-relying on coordinates without context.

**Delayed retention check:** After several days, describe a different public location using the same structure.

**Transfer task:** Use the format in a fictional emergency call and a routine meetup message, adjusting the detail level.

**Safety and scope:** Do not publish home address, live location or personal routine to strangers. Share precise location only with trusted recipients when necessary.

**Evidence to record:** Location source, description, listener repeat-back and ambiguity corrections.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-008 — Read a transit interchange diagram

**Target performance:** Interpret a station or interchange map to identify lines, platforms, transfer paths and service constraints.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use a current official transit diagram; practise route planning without travelling.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Read the legend and identify line colours/names and symbols. 2. Find the starting station and destination. 3. Trace the route in the correct direction and identify transfer stations. 4. Distinguish platform changes from station exits and street-level transfers. 5. Check whether transfer corridors, accessibility routes or separate ticket gates affect the journey. 6. Verify service disruptions and operating hours using current official information. 7. Count transfers and compare an alternative route for reliability.

**Practice dose:** Plan two journeys on a current map; include one with at least one interchange.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly identify lines, direction, transfer station and exit/boarding sequence for both journeys; verify current operating information.

**Common failure modes:** Reading only colours without labels; confusing interchange with same-platform transfer; missing separate fare gates; using outdated diagrams.

**Delayed retention check:** After one week, plan a route on a different network map and explain one symbol that differs.

**Transfer task:** Create a route handoff for a family member with transfer cues and a fallback.

**Safety and scope:** Actual travel must follow guardian guidance; do not run between platforms or cross restricted areas.

**Evidence to record:** Diagram source/date, route traced, transfers, service checks and fallback.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-009 — Maintain a situational check-in routine

**Target performance:** Use an agreed, proportionate check-in process during travel or activities so trusted people know expected plans and changes.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Agree the routine with a trusted adult; define when check-ins are appropriate and who receives them.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. State the activity, expected destination and approximate return/check-in time to the trusted person. 2. Agree on a simple arrival message and a reasonable missed-check-in response. 3. Keep the phone charged and contact method available where appropriate. 4. Send the check-in through the agreed channel without broadcasting it publicly. 5. If plans change, update the trusted person before the change creates uncertainty. 6. If a check-in is missed, follow the agreed escalation plan rather than assuming the worst. 7. Review whether the routine is proportionate and not intrusive.

**Practice dose:** Use during two ordinary supervised outings; do not create constant tracking for low-risk routine activities.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Trusted adult knows the expected plan and receives the agreed arrival/update message; privacy boundaries are respected.

**Common failure modes:** Sending live location publicly; forgetting to update after a route change; excessive check-ins that create false reassurance; relying on a dead phone.

**Delayed retention check:** Review after one month and after any change in route, device or trusted contact.

**Transfer task:** Create a check-in plan for a group project outing that balances safety and privacy.

**Safety and scope:** Location sharing is sensitive. Share only with trusted people, only as needed, and follow guardian rules; do not share passwords or public live location.

**Evidence to record:** Agreed plan, recipient, check-in completion, deviations and privacy review.

**Status:** Draft authored; independent subject review, learner testing and calibration required.


## MS-08-010 — Distinguish safe from unsafe shortcuts

**Target performance:** Evaluate whether a route shortcut is acceptable by considering visibility, access, traffic, lighting, isolation and permission rather than time alone.

**Prerequisites and setup:** Use map images or familiar supervised routes; never test a risky shortcut to see what happens.

**Acquisition sequence:**

1. Compare the official route with the proposed shortcut. 2. Check whether the path is public and permitted. 3. Consider traffic crossings, lighting, surface, water/height hazards and visibility. 4. Consider whether the route is isolated or has staffed public help nearby. 5. Check weather, time of day and whether the route remains open. 6. Reject shortcuts that cross private property, restricted areas or unsafe terrain. 7. Choose the safer route even if it takes longer and tell the trusted person if the plan changes.

**Practice dose:** Assess five route scenarios on a map; explain which factors outweigh time savings.

**Assessment and pass criterion:** Correctly rejects every scenario with a serious hazard or access violation and justifies the safer route using concrete criteria.

**Common failure modes:** Treating shortest distance as best; following crowds into restricted space; ignoring darkness/weather; assuming a familiar shortcut is always safe.

**Delayed retention check:** After a month, reassess a familiar route under a changed condition such as rain or construction.

**Transfer task:** Apply to cycling or school routes by adding mode-specific hazards and official route guidance.

**Safety and scope:** Minors must follow guardian-approved routes. Do not enter private, abandoned, construction or restricted areas.

**Evidence to record:** Scenario, route options, hazards, permission check, decision and reason.
