**Version:** 0.1 draft

## What this batch establishes

- A traceability crosswalk for all 216 baseline modules across 18 disciplines.
- Four task blueprints per discipline (72 total), with each task targeting a defined three-module cluster.
- A normalized inventory of existing assessment-bank items, preserving source text and explicitly marking unresolved links.
- A criterion-level 0–3 rubric proposal, evidence-package contract, safety gates and a calibration protocol.

This is an assessment *blueprint*, not a validated examination system. The 72 tasks are not calibrated tests, and module-to-task links are candidate instructional coverage.

## Rubric proposal

Each task is scored on five dimensions: accuracy, reasoning, application, evidence quality, and limits/reflection. Each dimension uses 0–3 anchors. Safety, consent, authorization, ethics and integrity gates are separate and cannot be averaged away. No universal pass threshold is imposed.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No usable evidence, materially incorrect, absent or unsafe response. |
| 1 | Partial evidence; major gaps or substantial prompting required. |
| 2 | Mostly correct and usable; minor omissions; reasoning explainable. |
| 3 | Accurate, well-reasoned, independently supported, context-aware and appropriately qualified. |

The scale remains provisional until discipline-specific exemplars, double scoring and learner pilots exist.

## Evidence contract

Every assessed event should preserve task/version, learner work, conditions/tools, source/data provenance, assistance disclosure, criterion-level scores and rationale, critical-gate outcome, evaluator role, uncertainty, learner reflection and any proposed update package. An AI answer alone is not learner evidence.

## Calibration sequence

1. Define the construct and observable evidence.
2. Independently double-score an anonymized sample.
3. Resolve criterion-level disagreement by revising anchors and examples.
4. Repeat on a fresh sample and preserve quantitative and qualitative agreement evidence.
5. Check accessibility, language load and tool effects separately.
6. Pilot delayed recall and transfer, not only immediate performance.
7. Promote criteria only after qualified review and learner validation.

## Discipline-specific safety boundaries

Safety gates are attached per discipline. High-risk areas include electrical work, cybersecurity, health/fitness, finance and household emergency response. Tasks are designed around fictional, low-risk or supervised scenarios; no real-world risky action is required.

## Unresolved issues carried forward

- Legacy assessment bank items need outcome-level links; several have no unit ID.
- Existing source banks use different schemas and score scales.
- No item difficulty, fairness, reliability, validity or answer-key audit has been completed.
- No independent subject review, double-rater calibration or learner pilot has occurred.
- Assessment criteria still need alignment to canonical skill/domain decisions and actual app evidence schema.
