# Resource Provenance and Research Standard v0.1
Status: Draft.

## 1. Minimum provenance
Record title, author/creator, publisher/host, resource type, URL or locator, publication/version date where available, access date, language, source class, license/usage notes and linked knowledge objects.

## 2. Source classes
Primary sources provide original data, records or direct evidence; secondary sources interpret/synthesise primary sources; tertiary sources organise summaries/reference material; instructional resources teach; tools and datasets are recorded distinctly. A source can be useful without being authoritative for every claim.

## 3. Verification states
`unreviewed`, `checked`, `corroborated`, `disputed`, `superseded`. Record what was checked and by whom. Do not mark a whole source universally reliable based on one checked claim.

## 4. Claim-level discipline
For consequential or contested claims, attach claim notes and supporting references. Distinguish source statements, interpretation and learner inference. Record limitations, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, recency and applicability.

## 5. Copyright and safety
Store metadata, notes and permitted excerpts rather than copying whole copyrighted works. Respect licenses and access controls. Safety-critical instructions require authoritative, current sources and explicit supervision/qualification notes.

## 6. Resource is not learning evidence
Opening, watching or completing a resource may support exposure tracking but does not demonstrate understanding or competence.
