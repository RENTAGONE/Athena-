# ATHENA — Cross-Domain Transfer and Financial Capability Framework
Version: 0.2.0  
Updated: 2026-11-16  
Status: Accepted strategic requirement; detailed curriculum and transfer registry remain to be built.

## 1. Primary outcome
ATHENA exists to develop broad, deep, transferable capability through the user's independent learning. It is not a school-management, homework, syllabus-tracking, tutoring, or exam-preparation application. The user manages school separately.

ATHENA should nevertheless create positive spillover into school and ordinary life. When the user independently encounters a school concept that overlaps with knowledge already developed through ATHENA, that prior learning should make the new material easier to understand. This is an indirect benefit of a coherent multidisciplinary education, not a separate school workflow or a promise of particular marks.

## 2. Cross-domain transfer system
ATHENA must represent meaningful relationships between knowledge across its 18 disciplines and foundational pillars. The knowledge graph should make it possible to see how one concept supports, enables, explains, or applies in another domain.

Examples:
- Mathematics foundations → physics equations, chemistry quantities, statistics, computing and engineering.
- Anatomy and physiology → biology, biomechanics, athletics, health literacy and human performance.
- Scientific reasoning → experiments, engineering investigations, research and evidence evaluation.
- Logic and analytical reasoning → mathematics, programming, strategy, decision-making and argumentation.
- Research literacy → independent learning, technical investigation, business research and source evaluation.
- Programming → automation, digital infrastructure, product development and engineering.
- Psychology → communication, negotiation, leadership, user research and business.

Use explicit, typed relationships where appropriate, such as `prerequisite_for`, `supports`, `applies_to`, `shared_foundation_for`, `enables`, `explains`, and `related_to`. Define direction and semantics; do not treat every association as a prerequisite. Do not create forced or superficial links merely to make the graph look interconnected.

The system should help the learner discover useful connections without requiring every lesson to mention every related discipline. Cross-domain transfer should be assessed through actual application in a different context, not assumed because two topics are linked.

## 3. Transfer evidence and incidental school benefit
ATHENA may record a transfer event when the user notices or demonstrates that ATHENA knowledge helped with an independent task outside the original learning context. A transfer record can include:
- source skill/concept and destination context;
- what the learner recognised or applied;
- whether the transfer was spontaneous, prompted, or deliberately practised;
- evidence (explanation, solution, project, observation or other artifact);
- confidence and date.

School may be one destination context among many, alongside projects, everyday decisions, conversations, sport, creative work and unfamiliar topics. ATHENA must not require school names, class schedules, syllabus mapping, textbook chapter tracking, homework tracking, exam calendars, grades or school-specific tutoring workflows. The user handles those separately.

The intended benefit is that the user may encounter a school topic and realise that ATHENA has already provided some of the mathematical, scientific, anatomical, linguistic or reasoning foundations. The system must not claim this guarantees high marks; marks depend on the actual curriculum, effort, instruction, assessment and execution.

## 4. Independent reading and learning capability
ATHENA should teach transferable methods for understanding unfamiliar material, regardless of whether it comes from a book, technical document, research source, course or school textbook. The learner should increasingly be able to:
1. Preview structure, purpose, diagrams and terminology.
2. Turn headings and objectives into questions.
3. Decode unfamiliar vocabulary, symbols, units and technical language.
4. Connect new information to prior knowledge and identify missing prerequisites.
5. Explain ideas in plain language and reconstruct examples independently.
6. Retrieve key ideas without looking and solve representative problems.
7. Apply concepts to unfamiliar contexts.
8. Diagnose errors and misconceptions.
9. Schedule retrieval and revisit fragile knowledge.

This is an ATHENA learning-to-learn capability, not a school-specific reading workflow. The goal is increasing independence rather than dependence on AI explanations.

## 5. Workload and real-life commitments
ATHENA should remain sustainable and should not displace sleep, health, relationships, school responsibilities, recovery or essential commitments. The user manages their own school activity; ATHENA's general planner may account for user-entered commitments if the user chooses, but it must not become a school administration system. Maintain minimum, normal and high-capacity modes, reduce optional learning during demanding periods, and avoid guilt-based streaks or backlog spirals.

## 6. Financial capability pathway
ATHENA should build financial capability progressively and ethically through:
- Personal finance: budgeting, saving, needs versus wants, emergency reserves, interest, inflation, risk and financial records.
- Business fundamentals: customer problems, value propositions, pricing, costs, revenue, profit, cash flow, unit economics, customer trust and retention.
- Execution: market research, product/service quality, distribution, sales, operations, documentation and responsible reinvestment.
- Financial reasoning: distinguish revenue, accounting profit, cash flow, assets, liabilities and personal savings; assess downside risk before committing money.
- Long-term development: accounting, economics, investing principles, diversification, valuation and capital allocation when prerequisites and legal eligibility are appropriate.

Financial stability is an outcome to work toward, not something ATHENA can guarantee. Emphasize durable earning ability, prudent money management, legitimate value creation and risk control—not speculative shortcuts, guaranteed returns or pressure to monetize every interest. As a minor, the user must follow applicable laws, platform age rules and guardian requirements for financial accounts, contracts and business activity.

## 7. Integrated capability without overload
Keep the full 18-discipline map available, but do not schedule all domains intensively at once. Use a portfolio of foundational learning, a small number of active focus domains, short continuity practices, integrated projects, exploration and maintenance/retrieval. Rotate focus based on prerequisites, motivation, evidence, real-life commitments and available time. Do not turn the system into a school-syllabus mirror.

ATHENA should create positive spillover into everyday life: clearer reading, better questions, stronger memory, more deliberate decisions, improved communication, practical problem-solving and independent learning. Record transfers when observed; do not assume transfer merely because a related lesson was completed.

## 8. Success criteria
Review at term and year boundaries using evidence such as:
- Breadth and depth of demonstrated skills across domains.
- Ability to independently learn unfamiliar material.
- Retention and application after a delay.
- Spontaneous and deliberate transfer across disciplines and real-life contexts.
- Completed projects and useful outputs.
- Quality of financial decisions, business literacy, legitimate value delivered, records and risk management; income is one measure, not the only one.
- Sustainability of learning alongside school, health, sleep, relationships and motivation.

Do not promise a particular grade, college admission, income or percentile. Use evidence, adapt the system and keep the user in control.
