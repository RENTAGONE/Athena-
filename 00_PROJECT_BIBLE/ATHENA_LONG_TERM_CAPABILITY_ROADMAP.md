# ATHENA Long-Term Human Capability Roadmap
Version: 0.1.0
Status: Strategic scenario, provisional; not a fixed promise or deadline.
Created: 2026-11-16

## Purpose
This roadmap describes plausible development horizons for ATHENA. It is a planning model, not a prediction, guarantee, or rigid curriculum. Progression depends on evidence, health, opportunity, practice quality, available time, and changing interests.

## Governing model
ATHENA should develop breadth and depth together:
- Breadth: build useful foundations and functional competence across many domains.
- Depth: select a small number of domains for sustained advanced study and original work.
- Integration: deliberately combine disciplines in projects.
- Transfer: learn to apply knowledge in unfamiliar contexts.
- Maintenance: retain important capabilities through retrieval and periodic practice.
- Adaptation: revise priorities as the learner's interests, circumstances and evidence change.

Do not attempt intensive study of all 18 disciplines simultaneously. Maintain a broad map, a small number of active priorities, and low-friction continuity practice in selected interests.

Possible focus:

Expected evidence: baseline-to-follow-up improvement, completed small projects, demonstrable foundational skills, consistent retrieval and a sustainable routine.

Possible focus:

### Years 4–5: 2029-12-10 to 2031-12-09 — Advanced competence and original work
Purpose: pursue advanced study in selected fields while maintaining breadth.
Possible directions:

- Visual communication, 3D and creative direction.

Expected evidence: original designs, products, research, collaborations, teaching, public portfolio and sustained performance.

### Years 6–10: 2031 onward — Independent capability and lifelong learning
Purpose: develop the ability to enter unfamiliar fields, learn efficiently, create original work, collaborate and transfer knowledge.
Possible outcomes:
- Learn unfamiliar technical fields systematically.
- Build products and solve complex problems.
- Make informed commercial and financial decisions.
- Conduct and communicate evidence-based research.
- Lead, negotiate, teach and collaborate.
- Create original technical and artistic work.
- Maintain physical health, capability and practical independence.
This is a direction of travel, not a promised outcome or completion date.

## How to measure progress
Do not use a single global score or claim a rank among all humans. Use evidence by domain:
- Knowledge: accurate explanation and retrieval.
- Skill: repeatable performance against defined criteria.
- Application: successful use in a real or realistic task.
- Transfer: ability to adapt in a new context.
- Retention: performance after a meaningful delay.
- Creation: original work, project outcomes or contribution.
- Collaboration: ability to work with and learn from others.
- Health and sustainability: safe practice, recovery and consistency.

Compare performance to explicit standards and suitable peer groups only when credible comparison data exists. Avoid fabricated percentiles and universal rankings.

## Breadth versus depth
Use a portfolio model:
- Core foundations: persistent, prioritized.
- Active focus domains: a small number at a time.
- Continuity lanes: brief practice that keeps important interests alive.
- Exploration slots: occasional sampling of new disciplines.
- Projects: integration across domains.
- Maintenance: spaced retrieval and periodic reassessment.

A discipline should not be considered complete merely because a course or book is finished. Likewise, a learner should not be forced to master every prerequisite in an entire discipline before applying useful knowledge.
